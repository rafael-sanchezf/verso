# Brand color: what the evidence says

Research notes, 2026-10-07. Scope: the evidence behind moving Verso from four per-medium colors
(games green, movies blue, books orange, TV purple) to one brand color. This file does not pick a
color. Every bullet carries a source and a tag.

**Tags.** `peer-reviewed` (journal or refereed proceedings), `industry study` (vendor or agency
research with a stated sample), `company` (a brand's or platform's own documentation), `opinion`
(blogs, design write-ups, secondary summaries). Two extra labels: `standard` for W3C normative
text, and `computed here` for arithmetic I ran with a cited method (the scripts are the WCAG 2.2
relative-luminance formula and the Machado 2009 color-vision-deficiency matrices; Verso's own
colors come from `packages/tokens/src/theme.ts`).

**Where a figure is secondhand**, the bullet says so. Several paywalled papers were read through
abstracts or through other papers that report them.

---

## 1. Distinctive brand assets and color

- Romaniuk defines distinctive assets as the non-name elements that trigger the brand for category
  buyers, scored on two axes: **fame** (share of category buyers who name your brand when shown the
  asset alone) and **uniqueness** (share of those who name only you).
  [OUP, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*, 2018](https://www.oup.com.au/books/higher-education/business-marketing/9780190311506);
  [perceptive.co.nz summary](https://www.perceptive.co.nz/blog/brand-distinctiveness-what-does-it-mean-for-you). `peer-reviewed` (book from the institute's research) / `opinion` (summary)
- **The grid.** Four quadrants: *Use or lose* (famous and unique), *Invest* (unique, not famous),
  *Avoid solo use* (famous, not unique: it cues competitors too), *Ignore or test* (neither). All
  new assets start in the last one. The book has a dedicated chapter (13) on colour-based assets.
  [Ehrenberg-Bass Brand Identity Q&A (PDF)](https://assets.marketingscience.info/Brand-Identity-QA.pdf);
  [SRH summary of the grid](https://srh.agency/dispatch/consistency-is-not-in-fact-the-hobgoblin-of-little-minds). `company` / `opinion`
- **Color is the weakest asset type.** Phua, Bali, Anesbury and Sharp (Ehrenberg-Bass), the first
  large benchmark: 1,000+ assets, 21 categories, 4 countries, 9 years. Shape-based assets (logos,
  packs) average 40% fame and 71% uniqueness; colour assets average **12% fame and 39%
  uniqueness**. Figures as reported by the authors on a podcast; the article is paywalled.
  [International Journal of Advertising, 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02650487.2026.2637295);
  [Marketing Architects episode with the figures](https://pod.wave.co/podcast/the-marketing-architects/nerd-alert-your-strongest-distinctive-brand-asset). `peer-reviewed`
- Same direction in packaged goods: across 1,281 assets in 13 categories, characters, logos and
  logotypes had the highest uniqueness; **colour and advertising style were the least unique**.
  Ward, Yang, Romaniuk and Beal, *Journal of Brand Management*, 2020, as reported in
  [Bournemouth automotive replication (PDF)](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/38862/1/Revised%20Manuscript%20-%20Clean%20Copy.pdf). `peer-reviewed`
- Automotive replication (44 de-branded UK assets): logo was the only reliably ownable type; 10 of
  11 colour assets had low uniqueness concentration. Same PDF as above. `peer-reviewed` (accepted manuscript)
- Ipsos's database of 12,000+ assets from 900+ brands: **only 15% reach "gold"** (instant, unique
  link to the right brand when shown alone); 1 in 5 brands has a strong enough asset portfolio.
  Combining visual, verbal and sonic cues strengthens attribution.
  [Ipsos, *Recognisably You*, 2026 (PDF)](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/publication/documents/2026-09/ipsos-voices-recognisably-you.pdf). `industry study`
- **Pair color with a shape.** Romaniuk's advice, in secondary summaries: colour needs support from
  a logo shape, design or wordmark to become distinctive (golden arches, the Guinness pint).
  A widely repeated line that "only around 4% of tested colors" evoke the right brand alone appears
  in a blog summary of the book; **I could not find the primary figure.**
  [tianpan.co book summary](https://tianpan.co/blog/2025/09/01/building-distinctive-brand-assets-by-jenni-romaniuk). `opinion`
- The recommended way to measure a colour link is colour-cued and unprompted ("which brand do you
  think of?"), not brand-prompted; prompting inflates scores. 880 consumers, three categories.
  [Romaniuk and Nenycz-Thiel, *Journal of Advertising Research*, 2014](https://www.warc.com/content/article/jar/measuring-the-strength-of-color-brand-name-links-the-comparative-efficacy-of-measurement-approaches/en-gb/102796). `peer-reviewed`
- **Do not try to own a category code.** Romaniuk: if a category's code is already in buyers'
  memories, ignoring it is unwise, but owning it is "hard work" because you compete with both the
  code's meaning and every other brand using it.
  [Ehrenberg-Bass, Q&A: Seven costly sins of brand identity](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/qa-seven-costly-sins-of-brand-identity). `company`
- **Time to attribution.** No peer-reviewed figure found. The most-cited number, "two to three years
  of consistent exposure," is a practitioner attributing it to Ehrenberg-Bass on a podcast.
  [Marketing Architects](https://pod.wave.co/podcast/the-marketing-architects/so-you-think-youre-distinctive). `opinion`.
  Romaniuk's own framing is that building is never finished and unused assets decay ("use or lose").
  [Brand Identity Q&A (PDF)](https://assets.marketingscience.info/Brand-Identity-QA.pdf). `company`
- A real-world benchmark of how slow and partial colour ownership is: after use since a 1995
  filing, 60% of Dutch respondents linked magenta to Deutsche Telekom in telecom, yet the Benelux
  office **invalidated the colour mark in August 2024** for lack of proof in Belgium and Luxembourg.
  [Wolters Kluwer trademark blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/trademark-blog/acquired-distinctiveness-for-non-traditional-trademarks-in-the-benelux-show-your-true-colours/). `opinion` (legal commentary on a public decision)

## 2. What hues signal

- **Labrecque and Milne 2012**, four studies. Hue maps onto Aaker's brand-personality dimensions:
  white and yellow → sincerity; red → excitement; blue → competence; black, purple, pink →
  sophistication; brown → ruggedness. Saturation and value amplify traits (in secondary summaries,
  higher saturation reads more exciting and rugged). Purchase intent is highest when the colour
  matches the personality the brand claims. **Green is not among the headline mappings**; I could
  not get the full text to confirm what green loaded on.
  [Springer abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-010-0245-y);
  [mappings as summarized by ResearchGate/secondary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251277565_Exciting_red_and_competent_blue_the_importance_of_color_in_marketing). `peer-reviewed`
- **Follow-up: iconic colours carry learned meaning.** Cadbury purple read as more *sincere* than
  generic purple; Apple's grey primed *excitement* for unknown brands; priming works best when the
  colour fits category expectations. Colour meaning is partly built by the brand, not fixed by hue.
  [Baxter, Ilicic and Kulczynski, *Journal of Brand Management*, 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/jobman/v25y2018i4d10.1057_s41262-017-0086-9.html). `peer-reviewed`
- **Singh 2006** ("Impact of color on marketing", *Management Decision* 44(6)) is a short literature
  review, not an experiment. Its famous line, that people judge a product within 90 seconds and
  62–90% of that judgement is colour, traces to "CCICOLOR – Institute for Color Research" as cited on
  Jill Morton's colorcom.com; **no underlying study is published.**
  [Emerald record](https://www.emeraldinsight.com/doi/abs/10.1108/00251740610673332);
  [colorcom.com attribution](https://www.colorcom.com/research/why-color-matters/). `peer-reviewed` (review) / `opinion`
- **Cross-cultural.** 4,598 people in 30 nations: colour-emotion associations are largely universal
  (average similarity r = .88), stronger between linguistically or geographically close nations.
  Green's associations were among the **most consistent** across nations, and green did not appear
  among the strong (p ≥ 0.4) colour-emotion pairs: it carries no single dominant emotion.
  [Jonauskaite et al., *Psychological Science*, 2020 (open PDF)](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/9c8b18e0-3848-43f5-838b-5035438cfd12/content). `peer-reviewed`
- Eight-country preference study: blue was the top-ranked colour in Austria, China and the US;
  green ranked in the top five alongside white, black and red.
  [Madden, Hewett and Roth, *Journal of International Marketing*, 2000](https://ixdf.org/literature/author/thomas-j-madden). `peer-reviewed`
- Known cultural exceptions for green (sacred in Islam; socially loaded in some Chinese contexts)
  are reviewed in [Aslam, *Journal of Marketing Communications*, 2006](https://ro.uow.edu.au/commpapers/1043). `peer-reviewed` (review; full text not read)
- **Green as "eco" is weaker than assumed.** Logo colour shifts ethical judgements of a retailer via
  perceived eco-friendliness, but **blue read as more eco-friendly than green**.
  [Sundar and Kellaris, *Journal of Business Ethics*, 2017, via University of Oregon](https://business.uoregon.edu/news/study-suggests-color-affects-brand-judgment). `peer-reviewed`
- Green used on its own (without an eco claim) **lowered perceived product efficacy** across three
  experiments: it creates category ambiguity.
  [Pancer, McShane and Noseworthy, *Journal of Business Ethics*, 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jbuset/v143y2017i1d10.1007_s10551-015-2764-4.html). `peer-reviewed`
- Green as "money": Robinhood's agency described its bright green as "a subtle and optimistic play
  on the color of money."
  [Google Design, Robinhood case](https://design.google/library/robinhood-investing-material). `company`
- Green as "sustainability signal" is exactly the move Romaniuk warns against: brands pick green to
  look sustainable, end up sharing it, and the meaning competes with the brand link.
  [Romaniuk, Marketing Week, 2025-09-09](https://www.marketingweek.com/jenni-romaniuk-meaning-distinctive-assets/). `opinion` (by the researcher)
- In tech, a 2022 test with a fictitious brand found the green logo variant scored slightly higher
  on emotional connection (mean 3.20 vs blue 3.08, red 3.07); one fictitious brand, small gaps.
  [Hotwire and University of Sydney, *Decoding Tech Brands*, 2022 (PDF)](https://www.sydney.edu.au/content/dam/corporate/documents/news-opinions/2022/hotwire-australia-2022---decoding-tech-brands-research-paper.pdf). `industry study`

## 3. Category color codes and differentiation

- **Match or break: it depends on the category.** 281 leading brands, 15 categories: colour
  differentiation helps in some categories and hurts in others; conforming pays where there is a
  dominant leader, especially in high-involvement categories.
  [Labrecque and Milne, *Marketing Letters*, 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/mktlet/v24y2013i2p165-176.html). `peer-reviewed`
- Atypical package colours raised scepticism and lowered interest and purchase intent in two
  experiments (packaged goods, not apps).
  [Garaus and Halkias, *Review of Managerial Science*, 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/rvmgts/v14y2020i5d10.1007_s11846-018-0325-9.html). `peer-reviewed`
- **Appropriateness.** Functional products are judged more appropriate in "functional" colours and
  sensory-social products in "sensory-social" colours; when people know a brand's intended
  position, congruent colours look more appropriate. Secondary summaries place blue and green on
  the functional side and red, yellow, pink on the sensory-social side; the abstract I could read
  confirms only the congruity principle.
  [Bottomley and Doyle, *Marketing Theory*, 2006](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/2579/). `peer-reviewed`
- **"Blue is the tech default."** Best-sourced version: about 50% of tech brands at every stage
  (scaleups, listed, global leaders) use blue, versus green, red, orange or black for the rest; the
  study calls it a "sea of sameness."
  [Hotwire and University of Sydney, 2022 (PDF)](https://www.sydney.edu.au/content/dam/corporate/documents/news-opinions/2022/hotwire-australia-2022---decoding-tech-brands-research-paper.pdf). `industry study`
  Canva counted blue in 43% of Fortune 500 logos.
  [Transform, 2019](https://www.transformmagazine.net/articles/2019/blue-tops-list-of-most-used-colour-in-fortune-500-logos/). `industry study`
- **The often-quoted "blue in 33% of top 100 brands"** (red 29%, black 28%, yellow 13%) traces to
  2013 agency infographics (TastyPlacement on a Forbes list; 123Print) with no published method.
  [designbeep, 2013](https://designbeep.com/2013/04/17/the-colors-and-fonts-used-in-the-top-brand-logos-infographic/);
  [The Drum, 2013](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2013/08/26/infographic-what-s-brands-logo). `opinion`.
  A 2024 recount of Forbes' top 100 found blue and red each in 37%, green in 8%.
  [Martin for Hire](https://martinforhire.com/blog-post/colors-in-logo-design-a-case-study-of-the-100-most-valuable-brands/). `opinion`
- **Challengers that broke a code** (caution: none of these isolates colour from strategy):
  - Monzo's hot coral began as a rushed beta-card colour and became its defining asset in a
    category of navy and grey; cards drew comments at bars and barriers.
    [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/is-hot-coral-a-feature-or-a-liability-a-closer-look-at-the-colour-of-your-bank-card-fbd2f987d359). `opinion`
  - T-Mobile magenta: registered by Deutsche Telekom in 2000, enforced hard (Intel Centrino), yet
    still invalidated in Benelux (section 1).
    [KUOW/NPR, 2019](https://www.kuow.org/stories/t-mobile-has-a-trademark-on-magenta-demands-an-insurance-company-stop-using-it). `opinion`
  - Headspace kept orange through its 2024 rebrand to stand out from mental health's "dreary sea of
    blues and greys."
    [It's Nice That](https://Itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424). `opinion` (quoting the company)
  - Robinhood 2024: "a focused color palette, rooted in black, white and neutrals, with purposeful
    pops of Robin Neon," a yellow-green "unique to Robinhood," against competitors' rainbow palettes.
    [Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/a-new-visual-identity);
    [Porto Rocha](https://portorocha.com/robinhood). `company`
- **Green is already claimed near Verso's categories** (facts, not a recommendation):
  Letterboxd's action and rating colour is green (#00E054)
  [blakecrosley.com design guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/letterboxd) `opinion`;
  Xbox green (#107C10) since 2001
  [Xbox Brand Guidelines 2019 (PDF)](https://retrocdn.net/images/c/c0/Xbox_BrandGuidelines_2019-04-15.pdf) `company`;
  Razer green (Pantone 802 C)
  [Razer newsroom](https://www.razer.com/newsroom/product-news/razer-esports-green-collection) `company`;
  Hulu green (#1CE783) [This is Hulu](https://www.thisishulu.com/) `company`;
  Spotify green [Spotify for Developers](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design) `company`;
  Duolingo Feather Green [Duolingo design](https://design.duolingo.com/identity/color) `company` (page now redirects).

## 4. One color versus many in product design

- **Apple HIG, Branding** (refined 2026-09-09): "Apply your app's accent color judiciously. Using your
  brand color too broadly can overwhelm your interface and dilute its impact." Use it for primary
  actions or status indicators (unread badges, the selected tab); express brand in the content layer
  under Liquid Glass; branding defers to content; don't repeat the logo.
  [developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding). `company`
- **Apple HIG, Color**: "Avoid using the same color to mean different things", e.g. if the brand
  colour marks a button as interactive, don't use it on non-interactive text. In apps with mostly
  monochrome content, "choosing your brand color as the app accent color can be an effective way";
  with colourful content (box art, posters), prefer monochrome bars or a well-differentiated accent.
  Supply light, dark and increased-contrast variants. Don't redefine semantic system colours.
  [developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color). `company`
- **Material 3** gives three accent groups (primary, secondary, tertiary), each with *on-*,
  *container* and *on-container* roles "to represent your brand," and separate roles that "don't
  represent your brand" (error, surfaces, outline). Name brand colours literally (`brand_blue`),
  not after slots.
  [Material Components Android, Color.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Color.md);
  [m3.material.io color roles](https://m3.material.io/styles/color/roles). `company`
- **Many colours imply variety.** Four experiments: a more colourful logo makes people infer the
  brand offers more product variety; that lifts attitudes for *general* (one-stop) positioning but
  not for *specialized* positioning.
  [Song, Xu and Jiang, *Psychology & Marketing*, 2022 (accepted PDF)](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3166637/1/Color%20PM3%20Main%20Document%20Final.pdf). `peer-reviewed`
- **Palette reductions.** Slack 2019 (Pentagram): from 11 colours to 4 plus aubergine; the old mark
  "was 11 different colors" and "looked terrible" on any background but white or at the wrong angle.
  [Slack blog](https://slack.com/blog/news/say-hello-new-logo); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/01/17/slack-logo-rebrand-pentagram/amp/). `company` / `opinion`.
  Apple moved from the rainbow logo to monochrome in 1998; Microsoft went the other way in 2012
  (noted in Song et al. 2022, above). `peer-reviewed` (as background)
- **One-colour brands and how they spend it:**
  - Spotify: the green logo "should only be used with black, white, and non-duotoned photography";
    otherwise use black, white or monochrome logos.
    [Spotify for Developers](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design). `company`
    The product is near-black surfaces with green reserved for play, active states and primary
    calls to action. [design reference](https://www.shadcn.io/design/spotify/raw). `opinion`
  - Xbox: green is used as a thin "green bar" (1 to 1.5% of layout width plus height) on otherwise
    white or black layouts. [Xbox Brand Guidelines 2019 (PDF)](https://retrocdn.net/images/c/c0/Xbox_BrandGuidelines_2019-04-15.pdf). `company`
  - Robinhood 2024: neutrals with "pops" of one neon (section 3). `company`
  - Letterboxd is the multi-colour exception: three dots (orange, green, blue) in the mark, with
    roles split in product: green for actions and ratings, orange for Pro, blue for links.
    [Letterboxd brand assets](https://letterboxd.com/about/brand/) `company`;
    [role split per blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/letterboxd) `opinion`
  - Beli: brand colour not publicly documented; reviewers note a teal "saved" state and score flags
    that run green (high) to red (low).
    [Pratt IXD critique, 2022](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2022/02/design-critique-beli-ios-app/). `opinion`
  - Cash App: green used as a full-bleed canvas, with black pill buttons rather than green buttons.
    [design reference](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/cash-app/). `opinion` (low confidence)
- **Spotify's text on green is black, and the arithmetic explains why.** #1ED760 has relative
  luminance 0.50: black on it is 10.9:1, white is 1.9:1. Any colour with luminance above 0.179 gets
  more contrast from black than white. Spotify's play glyph and primary labels on green are black
  (observed; the style references above say the same). `computed here` with the
  [WCAG 2.2 formula](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-relative-luminance) `standard`

## 5. Accessibility constraints

- **WCAG 2.2 AA:** text 4.5:1; large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold; about 24 px / 18.5 px) 3:1;
  **logotypes exempt**. UI components and meaningful graphics 3:1 against adjacent colours (1.4.11).
  Colour cannot be the only carrier of meaning (1.4.1).
  [Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html);
  [WCAG 2.2, 1.4.11 and 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast). `standard`
- **Apple's Accessibility Inspector thresholds** are looser for bold: up to 17 pt 4.5:1; 18 pt 3:1;
  **bold at any size 3:1**. Apps that miss these should at least pass with Increase Contrast on.
  [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility). `company`
- **Why green is hard (computed).** At full saturation, the lightest shade that carries white text
  at 4.5:1 is about HSL lightness 27% for green (#008A17), versus 51% for blue and 62% for purple.
  At 3:1 (bold) green reaches 34% (#00AD1D). A green bright enough to feel fresh fails white text;
  one dark enough for white text sits in forest territory. Yellow and teal behave like green.
  `computed here` ([WCAG formula](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-relative-luminance))
- **How the green brands resolve it** (contrast `computed here`; the colour choices are sourced):

  | Brand green | White on it | Black on it | What the brand does | Source |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Spotify #1ED760 | 1.92 | 10.94 | Black glyphs and labels on green; green logo only on black, white or photos | [Spotify for Developers](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design) `company` |
  | Xbox #107C10 | 5.37 | 3.91 | "Use white type when using green"; a dark green that passes | [Xbox guidelines 2019](https://retrocdn.net/images/c/c0/Xbox_BrandGuidelines_2019-04-15.pdf) `company` |
  | Duolingo #58CC02 | 2.09 | 10.05 | White labels on green buttons (fails 4.5 and 3:1) | [design reference](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/duolingo/) `opinion` |
  | Hulu #1CE783 | 1.64 | 12.81 | Green logo on dark; black logo when the background is green | [This is Hulu](https://www.thisishulu.com/) `company` |
  | WhatsApp #25D366 | 1.98 | 10.59 | White glyph on green in the logo (exempt); older assets used dark teal #075E54 (white 7.67) | [color.codes](https://color.codes.in/brands/whatsapp) `opinion` |

  On dark surfaces every bright green above clears 4.5:1 as text or icon colour (Spotify on #121212
  is 9.8:1). `computed here`
- **Prevalence of red-green colour deficiency:** about 8% of men and 0.4% of women of European
  descent; 4–6.5% of Chinese and Japanese men.
  [Birch, *JOSA A* 29:313, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.29.000313). `peer-reviewed`
- **Gaming skew is real but genre-dependent.** US players overall: 52% men, 47% women (ESA 2025,
  YouGov, n = 5,000) [wnhub summary](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-47957) `industry study`.
  Across 270,000 core gamers, women are about 70% of match-3 and farm-sim players but about 7% of
  shooter and 2% of sports players.
  [Quantic Foundry via Kotaku, 2017](https://kotaku.com/study-shows-which-video-game-genres-women-play-most-1791435415). `industry study`.
  Implied CVD share: about 4% of a 52/47 audience, about 6.5% of an 80% male one. `computed here`
- **What a green brand looks like under deuteranopia** (Machado model, full dichromacy):
  it does not disappear; it keeps its lightness, loses about half its chroma and turns khaki or
  olive. Spotify #1ED760 → #C6B669; Xbox #107C10 → #74681F; Apple systemRed #FF3B30 → #A99725.
  Green brand and red error stay apart mainly by lightness (OKLab distance falls from 0.40 to 0.10).
  `computed here`, method: [Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes, *IEEE TVCG*, 2009](https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html) `peer-reviewed`
- **The real risk is in Verso's current tier palette, not the brand.** The four tier colours share
  one lightness (OKLCH L 0.75), so hue is their only difference. Under deuteranopia "Loved it"
  #6AC56A → #BCAE70 and "Didn't like it" #FF8274 → #BFB172: **OKLab distance 0.009, effectively the
  same colour** (0.262 for normal vision). "Liked" and "Fine" fall to 0.024. `computed here`
  (tokens from `packages/tokens/src/theme.ts`; method as above). Apple: convey state "with more than
  color alone," naming red-green as a hard pairing.
  [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility). `company`

## 6. The semantic collision

- **Platforms separate brand from status.** Material: primary, secondary and tertiary represent
  the brand; error and other "additional colors" don't. Its harmonization API shifts "reserved"
  colours ("those used for semantic or brand") toward the primary hue while leaving them
  "recognizable"; error is harmonized by default.
  [Material Components Android, Color.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Color.md). `company`
- Apple keeps semantic system colours (systemGreen, systemRed) separate from the app accent, and
  says don't redefine their meanings or use one colour for two meanings.
  [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color). `company`
  It does allow the accent for status indicators like unread badges and the selected tab.
  [HIG Branding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding). `company`
- Atlassian lists brand, information, success, warning, danger, discovery and accent as distinct
  roles; "Don't use an accent when the color has semantic meaning."
  [Atlassian Design, Color](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color). `company`
- **Brands whose primary equals a status colour, and how they live with it:**
  - Robinhood: brand green in onboarding, while green/red mark up/down; the screen itself shifts
    with market state (white during hours, grey when closed). The status layer *is* the brand's
    meaning ("color of money").
    [Google Design](https://design.google/library/robinhood-investing-material). `company`.
    Critics argued the red-on-down design could prompt panic selling.
    [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/robinhood-says-users-have-made-25-billion-through-trades-on-its-platform-but-one-influential-retail-investor-warns-its-easy-to-use-interface-could-spark-panic-selling-if-stocks-take-a-tumble/). `opinion`
  - Duolingo: Feather Green is both the brand and the "correct" state; red marks mistakes.
    [design reference](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/duolingo/). `opinion`
  - Spotify: since 2023 adding a track to Liked Songs turns the "+" into a **green checkmark**:
    brand colour as the affirm state.
    [SamMobile, 2023](https://www.sammobile.com/news/spotify-heart-button-replaced-with-an-improved-plus-button/). `opinion`
  - Letterboxd: green for watched, liked and ratings ("action and affirmation"); orange kept for paid.
    [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/letterboxd). `opinion`
  - Beli: a teal accent for saves, green-to-red for scores, so brand and score scale differ.
    [Pratt IXD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2022/02/design-critique-beli-ios-app/). `opinion`
- Pattern across these cases (my reading, not a source): the collision is tolerated when the brand

  existing gap is Verso's own games green #439550 vs "Loved" #6AC56A (OKLab 0.15). `computed here`

## 7. App icon colour and store performance

- **Evidence quality: low.** What exists is vendor case studies (single apps, no controls published,
  winners chosen by the vendor), plus counts of what top apps use. I found **no published,
  peer-reviewed or methodologically documented study that isolates icon hue** in games or
  entertainment. Treat every number below as anecdote.
- SGN's Cookie Jam: changing only the icon's background colour lifted conversion 8% (Google Play
  experiments). [Android Developers Blog, 2015](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2015/12/android-developer-story-sgn-game-cookie.html). `company`
- Hobnob: a brighter, deeper icon beat the post-rebrand icon by 64% and restored pre-rebrand
  conversion: a rebrand that lowered salience cost installs.
  [SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/customers/hobnob-app-optimizing-with-splitmetrics/). `industry study` (vendor case)
- Azur Games: red armour beat blue by 20–23%, and did better on a muted background than a bright
  one; the authors read it as contrast, not hue.
  [Azur Games blog](https://azurgames.com/blog/how-changing-app-icon-affects-conversion-to-installs). `industry study` (publisher case)
- SplitMetrics (about 200 apps, 3,500+ tests, 2024): icon work can lift installs up to 25%; simple
  backgrounds help; finance apps gained 12% from bold colours. No games breakdown.
  [MarTech Cube summary](https://www.martechcube.com/splitmetrics-research-app-icon-optimisation-lands-up-to-25-more-users/). `industry study`
- "Warm colours win in games and entertainment" circulates **without a source**.
  [appalize ASO blog](https://github.com/appalize/aso-blog/blob/main/app-design/app-icon-ab-testing-data-driven-design.md). `opinion`
- What top games use: black was the most common icon colour among the **top 10** iOS games
  (Semrush, Feb 2022). [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1296126/icons-colors-top-ios-apps-by-category). `industry study` (tiny sample).
  Earlier counts found white and blue dominant across categories.
  [Jared Sinclair, 2014](https://jaredsinclair.com/2014/08/08/whats-the-most-common-app-icon-c.html). `opinion`
- Dark icon with bright mark vs flat colour field: **no comparative data found** for games or
  entertainment. Apple's own guidance: a simple solid or gradient background that emphasizes the
  foreground; colour backgrounds give the most contrast in dark icons; design default, dark and
  tinted variants. [HIG App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons). `company`
- The store lets you test this directly: up to three alternate icon treatments in Product Page
  Optimization, for up to 90 days; alternate icons must ship in the binary.
  [Apple, Product Page Optimization](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/). `company`

## 8. Name and colour coherence

- **Romaniuk argues against choosing assets for meaning.** Meaning reduces uniqueness (rivals pick
  the same green for sustainability), adds mental competition while the link is being built,
  limits where the asset can be used, and "can put an expiry date on the asset" when the brand
  expands or shifts direction.
  [Marketing Week, 2025-09-09](https://www.marketingweek.com/jenni-romaniuk-meaning-distinctive-assets/). `opinion` (by the researcher)
- The counter-evidence is congruence research: brand names with front vowels are perceived as
  smaller, lighter, faster, and congruent name-mark pairings communicate meaning better.
  [Klink, *Marketing Letters*, 2003](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226373634_Creating_Meaningful_Brands_The_Relationship_Between_Brand_Name_and_Brand_Mark). `peer-reviewed`
  A student replication found no liking gain from congruent logos.
  [University of Twente thesis](https://essay.utwente.nl/essays/77995). `opinion`
- Consumers hold strong views on which colours suit which corporate images.
  [Hynes, *Journal of Brand Management*, 2009](https://figshare.utas.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Colour_and_meaning_in_corporate_logos_An_empirical_study/22963670). `peer-reviewed`
- **Context only, not a recommendation.** "Verso" is the back or left-hand side of a leaf, from
  Latin *versō foliō*, "on the turned leaf."
  [Wikipedia, Recto and verso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recto_and_verso). `opinion`.
  Colours conventionally tied to the page: black text with red for headings and section starts
  (rubrication, from Latin *ruber*), later also blue and green inks.
  [Wikipedia, Rubrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubrication). `opinion`.
  Penguin's 1935 covers colour-coded by genre: orange fiction, **green crime**, dark blue
  biography, cerise travel, red drama: a historical precedent for per-category colour inside one brand.
  [Wikipedia, Penguin Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Books);
  [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/article/classic-penguins-how-minimalist-book-covers-sold-paperbacks-to-the-masses/). `opinion`
