# Onboarding flows: Letterboxd, Criticker, Taste.io, MovieLens, Beli (as of 2026-10-01)

Raw note from a research sub-agent of the first (stalled) inventory run on 2026-10-01, kept because its findings are dated and sourced. Method caveats from the agent: its web search budget ran out partway; web.archive.org and reddit.com failed at the tool level; criticker.com and taste.io returned 403 on every path; movielens.org renders client-side and returned empty shells. Beli, the priority, was not materially limited.

## Letterboxd

- Flow: (1) the whole site is browsable with no account; (2) create an account with email, username, and password (full field list unconfirmed); (3) no genre, decade, or favorites prompt anywhere in sign-up; (4) the account is live with an empty diary; (5) "Four Favorites" on the profile is a discoverable feature set whenever the user likes, not a gated screen.
- Format: text fields; Four Favorites is a free-text title search, not chips or a grid.
- Minimum items required: none. Skippable: everything past having an account, and browsing needs none.
- Imports: none at sign-up; social sign-in unconfirmed.
- Asked later: Four Favorites; Pro-subscriber genre stats; Nanocrowd-powered "nanogenre" suggestions on film pages during browsing (partnership from March 2022).
- Screens before first useful result: 1 to 2 to a usable account, and no algorithmic personalized result by design.
- Company statement: "while we view the whole of Letterboxd as one big, organic recommendation engine, we don't have a section specifically labeled as such" ([Letterboxd FAQ](https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/), undated, fetched 2026-10-01). No metrics.
- Sources: company statement, the FAQ above; reference, [Wikipedia, Letterboxd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letterboxd), accessed 2026-10-01; company statement, [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/letterboxd/id1054271011), retrieved 2026-10-01.
- Gaps: the live sign-up page and a Behance case study returned 403; social sign-in and any post-sign-up nudge toward Four Favorites unconfirmed.

## Criticker

- Flow (best reconstruction): sign-up form (fields unconfirmed); free-form search and rate; at the tenth rating the matching engine activates.
- Format: search and rate, not chips, a grid, or a deck; the rating widget unconfirmed.
- Minimum items required: ten ratings before matching ("the matching doesn't start until you hit at least 10 ratings... As soon as you rate your tenth title, Criticker will start working"; company statement via search index, consistent with the repo's 2026-09-24 research).
- Skippable: assessed as a functional threshold rather than a blocking UI gate (the page "Rate More Films To Get More Recommendations" reads as a nudge); an inference, not a visual confirmation.
- Imports: CSV, IMDb, and Letterboxd imports advertised (carried over from the 2026-09-24 pass; not re-verified live).
- Asked later: accuracy framed as improving at 50 and again past 100 ratings (company FAQ content).
- Screens before first useful result: a sign-up screen then a repeating rate interface used about ten times.
- Company rationale: the floor exists because it "needs to be able to match you to other users" (company statement, criticker.com, re-surfaced 2026-10-01). No metrics.
- Sources: company statements via search index, [How to Get Started with Criticker](https://www.criticker.com/get-started-with-criticker/) and the [rate page](https://www.criticker.com/rate/); the repo's prior research for the import claim.
- Gaps: every direct fetch of criticker.com returned 403 (Cloudflare); a reader-proxy workaround hit the same check; form fields, any sequential quiz, and the rating UI unconfirmed. The least re-verified app in this batch.

## Taste.io

- Flow (best reconstruction): sign-up (method unconfirmed); "a few questions" at sign-up, today a swipe of specific titles as like or dislike to "calculate your taste"; add streaming services; optionally connect a partner for shared recommendations; recommendations "from the collective souls of people who share your unique taste".
- Format: a like-or-dislike swipe deck as the core rating interaction, framed as building a taste graph for cross-user matching; service selection presumably a logo checklist (unconfirmed); partner connect as a one-to-one pairing.
- Minimum items required: not stated ("a few questions"). Skippable: unconfirmed.
- Imports: streaming-service selection for where-to-watch; a partner pairing; no contacts import, social sign-in, or IMDb or CSV import found.
- Asked later: not documented.
- Screens before first useful result: about 3 to 4. Confidence low to medium.
- Company statements: a developer reply on the App Store, 2018-07-24: "upon answering the few questions when you signed up, the whole app is already personalized to you"; the current listing pitches it as "gender-neutral and with no commercial-bias". No metrics.
- Sources: company statement, [App Store listing, Taste: Movie and TV Suggestions](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/taste-movie-tv-suggestions/id1361180197), retrieved 2026-10-01 (found through Apple's search API after taste.io returned 403), including the 2018 developer reply.
- Gaps: taste.io 403 on every path; Google Play truncated; order, minimum, and skippability unconfirmed.

## MovieLens (groups-based onboarding)

- Flow: historically three stages: v0 required five ratings before entry; v3 required fifteen, ten at a time, from titles chosen for popularity excluding the top 50 to 150 (Harper and Konstan, ACM TiiS 2015); v4 (2014) replaced individual ratings with preferences for curated groups of movies, each shown as three representative films and three tags ("Disney animation") (Chang, Harper, Terveen, CSCW 2015; GroupLens blog by Shuo Chang, 2015-03-11, fetched directly). The current site still reads "sign up now", "rate movies, get personalized recommendations"; the register and groups screens could not be rendered. No GroupLens post on onboarding since 2015.
- Format: group preference cards (three movies plus three tags, rated as a unit), distinct from a search box, chips, or a deck.
- Minimum items required: historically strict (5, then 15); the v4 redesign's point was to replace a fixed count with a lower-effort format; the current count unconfirmed.
- Skippable: the old versions were not skippable (no homepage until the minimum was cleared); current skippability undocumented.
- Imports: none, ever; no mobile app (Apple's search API returned nothing for "movielens" on 2026-10-01).
- Asked later: not documented; the platform front-loads elicitation by design as a research instrument.
- Screens before first useful result: about 2 to 4 for the 2015 design, which took "less than half the time" of the fifteen-rating flow.
- Company statement: "expressing preferences for groups of items requires less effort than expressing multiple preferences for individual items, for example, rating 'Disney animation' takes less effort than rating 'Frozen', 'The Lion King', and 'Bambi' individually" ([GroupLens blog, 2015-03-11](https://grouplens.org/blog/onboarding-new-users-in-recommender-systems/)), generalizable to any collaborative filter.
- Published metrics: the old flow took "over 5 minutes" and caused "dropout before users reached the homepage"; the group flow took less than half that time, users judged its initial lists "more useful", at the cost of "slightly reduced prediction accuracy" (same post and the CSCW 2015 paper).
- Sources: academic, [Harper and Konstan, The MovieLens Datasets, ACM TiiS 2015](https://files.grouplens.org/papers/harper-tiis2015.pdf) (from the 2026-09-24 pass); company statement and academic, the GroupLens blog post above; academic, Chang, Harper, Terveen, CSCW 2015 (referenced by the post); direct checks of movielens.org and Apple's search API, 2026-10-01.
- Gaps: the current registration and group screens could not be rendered; no evidence of a redesign since 2015 and no confirmation the design is unchanged.

## Beli

- Flow (synthesized; no single screen-by-screen source): (1) phone number first ("First, what's your phone number?", the first captured screen; screenshot archive, Lazyweb, updated May 2026); (2) profile setup, described as "a lot of details related to their profile and food preferences" (teardown, Pratt IxD, 2024-09-17); (3) city selection (medium confidence on placement); (4) rate a handful of restaurants already visited: free-text search by name, a three-way reaction ("I liked it / It was fine / I didn't like it"; Boston Globe, 2026-08-04), then a binary head-to-head against a restaurant already ranked, producing a 1 to 10 score (Wikipedia; a WineBerserkers user spent "a couple hours my first day on it rating restaurants"); (5) a friend-invite gate: a Continue button that does not advance until the user invites contacts, four per a 2024 teardown and a 2026-03-24 App Store review (no three-friend citation found this session); (6) cuisine exclusions and dietary tags exist as preferences, timing unconfirmed.
- Format: a numeric field; a city picker (no location-permission dialog documented); search-and-add plus a three-way tap plus a binary comparison, not a pre-populated grid, chips, or a swipe deck of suggestions; a contact-selection or share screen for invites (OS contacts permission versus a share link unconfirmed). The 2024 teardown notes the Continue button "appears disabled" yet triggers a popup nagging the user to invite friends.
- Minimum items required: four invites to clear the gate; then two further thresholds for other payoffs: ten reviews to unlock a "scores feature" (teardown, 2024-09-17) and thirty ratings with at least ten in one city for personalized recommendations and custom guides (Boston Globe, 2026-08-04). Three gates, three payoffs.
- Skippable: no clean skip exists and the block is broader than one statistic. "No steps can be skipped" (teardown, 2024-09-17, which proposes adding a skip); "forced to send an invite to 4 friends to continue" (App Store review, 2026-03-24); "Most features are locked behind sharing the app, inviting people, and jumping through hoops" (App Store review, 2026-05-24, from a user who installed and deleted the app three times over several years); 42 review mentions of forced invites, summarized as "aggressive onboarding requirements, specifically forcing contact sharing and friend invites to unlock basic features" (mwm.ai mirror, 2026-10-01); "the average score feature... you can only access it if you refer someone" (App Store review, Jul 19, likely 2026); "it made me invite 4 friends before using it... couldn't even think of 4 close friends to message" (Threads, 2025-10-20). Whether unaccepted invites satisfy the gate, or a hidden skip exists, is unconfirmed.
- Imports: the invite step is Beli's friend-finding, made a non-skippable onboarding step, the opposite of every other app in the batch. TikTok and Instagram can be linked to a profile, OpenTable for reservations, college IDs for campus features (Wikipedia; timing not pinned to onboarding). No history import of any kind.
- Asked later: cuisine exclusions and dietary tags (likely); the scores feature at ten reviews; recommendations and guides at thirty ratings; a friend-to-friend Match Score; continued unlocks (reservation priority, social bio linking, leaderboard) that grow with cumulative invites (Valley Magazine, 2025-04-17, carried over; the page now 404s).
- Screens before first useful result: about 4 to 5 to the first personal placement (phone, profile, city, one restaurant through reaction and comparison), before the invite gate; a social or comparative result sits behind both the four-invite gate and, for recommendations, thirty ratings, likely the most aggressive gate-to-value ratio of the five apps.
- Company statements: no source defends the "4 friends" figure by name. Founder Judy Thelen: "Trust is the most important thing to us", on showing only ratings from people you know and follow (Boston Globe, 2026-08-04). Wikipedia paraphrases the founders on "gamified ranking", invites raising leaderboard rank and unlocking features.
- Published metrics: none for the gate. Scale figures by date: "over 75 million global reviews across 30,000 cities as of September 2025" and "roughly 80% of users are under 35" (Wikipedia); "120 million ratings across 2 million establishments in 50,000+ cities" (Boston Globe, 2026-08-04). Funding: a 5.3M USD Series A in November 2023 (Goodwater), 12M USD total as of June 2025 including FirstMark (Wikipedia).
- Sources: teardown, [Design Critique: Beli App, Rohini Raj Rajan, Pratt IxD, 2024-09-17](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-beli-app/); teardown, [A Design Critique of Beli, Tanisha Naik, Pratt IxD, 2026-09-09](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/09/a-design-critique-of-beli-restaurant-discovery-and-social-platform/) (covers rating and lists, not onboarding); screenshot archive, [Lazyweb, Beli, updated May 2026](https://www.lazyweb.com/company/beli) (five of 68 screens visible without an account); reference, [Wikipedia, Beli (app)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_(app)), retrieved 2026-10-01; press, [Boston Globe, Janira Skrbkova, 2026-08-04](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/04/lifestyle/beli-restaurant-rating-app/); press, [Valley Magazine, 2025-04-17](https://www.valleymagazinepsu.com/foodies-unite-meet-beli/) (404 today; carried over); user reports, [mwm.ai mirror of App Store reviews](https://mwm.ai/apps/beli/1478375386), [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beli/id1478375386?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone), [WineBerserkers thread](https://www.wineberserkers.com/t/beli-app-for-rating-finding-restaurants/334219), [Threads post, 2025-10-20](https://www.threads.com/@fesshole/post/DQCTLXEiFSB/); company statement, [App Store description](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beli/id1478375386), retrieved 2026-10-01.
- Gaps: no first-hand walkthrough; the relative order of city, first ratings, and the invite screen is inferred; the contacts permission, unaccepted invites, and the timing of dietary prompts unconfirmed; no company defense of the figure.

## Cross-app notes

Beli is the outlier in making a social action a hard, non-skippable progression gate; every other app treats friend-finding, linking, or import as absent or optional. MovieLens's own history is the cautionary tale: a forced fifteen-rating minimum caused dropout before the homepage, fixed by cutting effort (group elicitation), not by adding gates, and Beli's 2024 to 2026 reviews show the same dynamic from the user's side. Beli's rating mechanic is search-and-add then head-to-head, not a swipe deck of suggested restaurants: no source describes Beli presenting a curated deck during onboarding, so Verso's deck of popular titles is its own design choice, with Taste.io's like-or-dislike swipe as the nearer precedent. Every app in the set defers its deepest personalization until after first activity. Letterboxd is the existence proof that a tracker can thrive with zero onboarding personalization and no recommender, a deliberate lower bound.
