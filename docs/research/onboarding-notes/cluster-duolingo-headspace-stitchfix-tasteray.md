# Onboarding flows: Duolingo, Headspace, Stitch Fix, and TasteRay (as of 2026-10-01)

Raw note from a research sub-agent of the first (stalled) inventory run on 2026-10-01, kept because its findings are dated and sourced, and because it includes live walkthroughs done in the browser the same day (duolingo.com/register, Headspace's web checkout funnel, Stitch Fix's sign-up to the account gate, and TasteRay's whole quiz), labeled "direct observation". The agent created no accounts and entered no payment details. Its web search budget ran out partway; web.archive.org and several pages (qz.com, help.headspace.com, a Headspace teardown on Medium, Duolingo's shareholder letter) were unreachable.

## Duolingo

- Flow (live walkthrough plus Page Flows): splash; "I want to learn..." (the page is internally titled "Learning Goal", so the language choice is the goal question); mascot interstitials; "How did you hear about us?"; experience level (new, or knows some: the latter is offered a placement test); "Why are you learning a language?"; a daily practice goal (discrete buttons; sources give 5, 10, 15, 20 or 5, 10, 15, 30 minutes); "select next step"; the first lesson, completed before any account exists (a soft wall); "account unlocked", then age, name, email, password as sequential screens; a hard wall after two or three lessons; a trial paywall; welcome, a tracking permission, Home.
- Format: single-select taps; the daily goal as labeled buttons, not a slider; profile fields sequential; the placement test a quiz.
- Minimum items required: a language; the first lesson before the account gate. Skippable: the placement test is optional; the account is deferred, then mandatory; whether the paywall is declinable was not reached.
- Imports: none applicable; social sign-in buttons not confirmed in today's flow.
- Asked later: notification permission incentivized through a reward chest; a widget prompt after some use; leagues; goal and reminder time editable; upsell copy experiments.
- Screens before first useful result: about 8 to 9 before a first templated lesson. Confidence medium; counts vary across teardowns (26 steps versus "38 screens").
- Company statements: "How We Learn How You Learn" (Burr Settles, 2016-12-14): the Half-Life Regression scheduler's A/B test, "9.5% increase in retention for practice sessions, a 1.7% increase for lessons, and... 12% increase for overall activity" over more than 12 million sessions; "Copy Testing and Experiments" (2022-01-14): an 8% rise in notification opt-in from one German copy test. Neither is about the onboarding screens; no Duolingo post about the goal or placement screens was found.
- Published metrics (teardown, relaunch.ai, 2026, reading Duolingo's investor materials, not verified against the filing): free-to-paid "grew from 3% to 8.9% over five years", about 1,200 experiments a year, delayed sign-up "+20% DAU", the soft-wall-then-hard-wall sequence "+8.2% DAU", streak protection "+2.1%" day 7 and "+4%" day 14.
- Sources: company statement, [How We Learn How You Learn, 2016-12-14](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-we-learn-how-you-learn/); company statement, [Copy Testing and Experiments, 2022-01-14](https://blog.duolingo.com/copy-testing-experiments/); direct observation, duolingo.com/register, 2026-10-01; teardown, [Onboarding on Duolingo, Page Flows](https://pageflows.com/post/ios/onboarding/duolingo/); teardown, [Duolingo Onboarding Teardown: 7 A/B Tests, relaunch.ai, 2026](https://relaunch.ai/blog/duolingo-onboarding-teardown-7-b-tests-behind-their-9-conver.html); teardown, [Choice Hacking, 2023-05-25](https://www.choicehacking.com/2023/05/25/how-duolingo-used-psychology-to-make-learning-addictive/); teardown, [The Product Folks, 2025-04-25](https://prodfolks.substack.com/p/tpf-nl-25th-april-2025).
- Gaps: the current daily-goal labels; social sign-in; paywall declinability; the investor figures unverified against the primary filing.

## Headspace

- Two flows. (A) The native app per growth.design: splash with a meditation video; a full sign-up form early; a multi-select goal screen; an animated "personalizing" transition; the paywall; the notification permission; Home. (B) The web "Try for 0 USD" checkout funnel, walked live on 2026-10-01: "Which goals should we work toward together?" first, before any sign-up; a benefits screen echoing the goals; a 40% discount modal; then payment, not reached. The goal question comes before sign-up on the web and after it in the app, a real discrepancy between entry points.
- Format: six checkboxes (confirmed by the accessibility tree): "Sleeping soundly", "Manage anxiety", "Reduce stress", "Be present and mindful", "Feel calm and relaxed", "Something else"; older write-ups show a different set, so the options have been revised.
- Minimum items required: none; the goal screen shows Skip and Continue side by side (direct observation). The paywall's dismissibility was not tested.
- Imports: none; consumer social sign-in not observed in the funnel tested; an enterprise SSO parameter exists on the login URL.
- Asked later: "Ebb", an "empathetic AI companion" giving "personalized content recommendations based on how you're feeling" (headspace.com), an ongoing check-in rather than a quiz; its cadence undocumented.
- Screens before first useful result: about 6 to 7 in the app (medium confidence, one teardown); 2 to 3 to the paywall on the web, which is a purchase funnel rather than a first recommendation.
- Company statement: none on the goal screen's design; headspace.com/ai and /science discuss AI safety and research, not onboarding. The teardown's critique: goals framed negatively and recommendations that do not always match the stated goal.
- Sources: direct observation, checkout.headspace.com, 2026-10-01; company statements, [headspace.com](https://www.headspace.com/) and [AI at Headspace](https://www.headspace.com/ai), accessed 2026-10-01; teardown, [Headspace user onboarding, growth.design](https://growth.design/case-studies/headspace-user-onboarding), undated; teardown (403, unverified), [tearthemdown on Medium](https://tearthemdown.medium.com/product-teardown-headspace-user-onboarding-personalisation-b6effd0df1d7).
- Gaps: help center blocked; the native flow not walked live; social sign-in and paywall skippability unconfirmed; no rationale or metrics from the company.

## Stitch Fix

- Flow (live to the account gate, then the company's help pages and engineering blog): "What can we style you for?" (Womens, Mens, Kids); "How would you like to get started?" (Google, Apple, email: the account gate, before any taste content); then, gated, the Style Profile ("size, fit preferences, style, lifestyle, and budget"), including the "style sphere" question of 24 outfit images answered yes or no in a two-column grid (engineering blog, 2016-11-30) and an optional Pinterest board URL near the end; order the first Fix; shipping and billing from the Account page; a stylist curates a box that ships days later.
- Format: single-select taps for the first two screens; an image multi-select grid for style; size and budget widgets unconfirmed.
- Minimum items required: the category and an account method, with no skip observed; the Style Profile's skippability unverified. The Pinterest connection is optional ("or invited to do so later").
- Imports: Google, Apple, email at the gate; Pinterest later and optional; payment last, after the quiz and the order.
- Asked later: "Style Shuffle", an ongoing thumbs-up or down rating of outfits with new collections released regularly (help center), the clearest progressive-profiling example in the whole set; "Stitch Fix Vision" similarly.
- Screens before first useful result: two confirmed gate screens plus a likely 5 to 10 screen profile, then a box days later; the first result is a physical shipment, not a screen.
- Company statements: the 2016-11-30 engineering post on how the 24-image grid won in testing ("faster and easier to scroll through"); CPO Tony Bacos on StyleFile (October 2024): "No one is simply one style type, so we developed an algorithm" from "billions of customer style submissions"; a "5% conversion uplift" after StyleFile (via press). Context: Q4 2024 revenue down 12.4% to 319.6M USD with a 35.7M USD loss.
- Sources: direct observation, stitchfix.com/signup to the account gate, 2026-10-01; company statement, [How do we capture style preferences during sign-up, 2016-11-30](https://multithreaded.stitchfix.com/blog/2016/11/30/us-design-capture-style-preferences-during-sign-up/); company statements, [How to create an account](https://support.stitchfix.com/support/solutions/articles/153000250443-how-to-create-an-account) and [Rate your style: play Style Shuffle](https://support.stitchfix.com/support/solutions/articles/153000250424-rate-your-style-play-style-shuffle), accessed 2026-10-01; press, [Glossy on StyleFile, October 2024](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/stitch-fix-introduces-ai-driven-customer-profiles-amid-revenue-decline/); press (403, via search only), Quartz on Style Shuffle.
- Gaps: the Style Profile screens were not observed (an account would have been required); the Pinterest step rests on snippets; the Quartz piece blocked.

## Wildcard: Achriom checked, TasteRay chosen

Achriom's sign-up is import and chat driven, with no questionnaire: "You start with your full collection, not a blank page" (achriom.com, 2026-10-01); users connect Goodreads, Letterboxd, Trakt, Apple Music, or AniList, or scan a shelf with the camera. So the wildcard is TasteRay (TasteRay, Inc., founders Mike Wolniak and Mike Jaskolski, offices in Wilmington and Cracow; no founding date on the site), an AI movie and TV "concierge" whose whole onboarding was walked live at try.tasteray.com on 2026-10-01, since no independent teardown of it exists.

- Flow (direct observation): (1) "What do you want from your picks?", a six-option multi-select; (2) an interstitial tailored to the picks (choosing "Picks for my taste, not the crowd's" surfaced a "92 of 100" stat) with "Next: name 3 titles you love"; (3) "Pick 3 titles you love", a search bar or a tap on one of about 36 popular posters in a static grid (not a swipe deck), exactly three slots, then a confirmation ("Three gut picks. Nice", with "swap one") and an AI-written taste summary (Interstellar, Breaking Bad, and Fight Club produced "You love cerebral stories that hit hard!"); (4) a "No go zone" of content exclusions (Jump Scares, Horror, Blood and Gore, Violence, Spiders, Clowns, Erotic, Torture, Needles, Snakes, Animal Harm, Self-harm, Abuse, Death) as chips, the only skippable step; (5) "What's on your mind lately?", a required free-text field of 300 characters ("Netflix doesn't know this about you!"), which generates an AI reply and sub-genre chips ("Just started a new job" produced "Workplace comedies", "Career underdogs"); (6) a one-title-per-screen rating loop labeled "Step 5 of 6" throughout, with seven buttons (Personal top, Love it, Liked it, Mixed feelings, Didn't like, Hated it, Haven't seen) and an optional note. The agent rated nine consecutive titles with the loop still at "Step 5 of 6" and no end in sight, against the site's own copy ("TasteRay never asks you to rate fifty movies first. A few is enough").
- Format: checkboxes; a tile grid plus search capped at three; chips; required free text; a seven-point sentiment button set per title. No sliders.
- Minimum items required: at least one goal; exactly three titles; non-empty text at step 5; a response per card at step 6, where "Haven't seen" counts.
- Skippable: only the content-warning step. Imports: none anywhere in the quiz. A sign-in link for returning users; no account forced mid-quiz; a paid tier (6.99 USD a month or 69.99 USD a year) is mentioned on the marketing site, and the paywall's position was not reached.
- Screens before first useful result: at least 16 observed (goal, interstitial, three titles, confirmation, summary, warnings, free text, AI reply, nine or more rating cards) without reaching the promised "3 picks worth your evening". Confidence low on the total, high on "longer than the copy says".
- Company statements: the methodology page says the engine "learns from user input combined with professional critic reviews", matching "the user's stated mood, occasion, and prior loves"; "92% of users discover a new personal favorite within their first month", from opt-in research on "4,200+ active users" between 2025-09 and 2026-04 on a 1 to 10 self-rating with 8 and above counted as a discovery, which the company itself says to "read as directional rather than as controlled findings".
- Sources: direct observation, try.tasteray.com, 2026-10-01; company statements, [TasteRay methodology](https://www.tasteray.com/methodology), [team](https://www.tasteray.com/team), [landing](https://www.tasteray.com/) and [try](https://try.tasteray.com/) pages, accessed 2026-10-01; company statement, [Achriom](https://www.achriom.com), accessed 2026-10-01.
- Gaps: no independent coverage of TasteRay exists; the final step, the account, and the paywall were not reached.

## Cross-app notes

Duolingo, Headspace, and Stitch Fix all open with some version of an intent question and route it very differently: Duolingo treats the language itself as the goal and branches hard on experience level (placement test or unit 1); Headspace's multi-select goal is skippable and, per the teardown, its recommendations do not reliably reflect it; Stitch Fix asks no "why" at all, gates on the account before any taste content, and substitutes a long structured profile plus an ongoing swipe loop (Style Shuffle). TasteRay is structurally the closest of any app studied to Verso's plan (a goal multi-select, "pick 3 titles you love", then a one-at-a-time rating loop), and its most actionable lesson is the gap between its promise ("a few is enough") and its behavior (nine or more cards with no end), a concrete caution for Verso's deck to feel short rather than be advertised as short. TasteRay also routes on generated text mid-onboarding (the free-text step reshaping the genre chips), which none of the three canonical apps attempt.
