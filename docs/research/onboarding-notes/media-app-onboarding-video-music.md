# Media app onboarding inventory: video, music, and adjacent trackers, as of 2026-10-01

Research compiled 2026-10-01. Every claim is dated to the source's own date, not to today, and labeled as one of: **(company)** the company's own page, blog, newsroom, help center, or a named employee; **(teardown)** a UX teardown site, press analysis, or reverse-engineering writeup; **(user report)** an App Store/Play review, Reddit/forum thread, or other end-user account. Where a primary page blocked automated fetches, the Wayback Machine capture is cited and said so. This note is in progress; it is written early and extended in batches so an interruption loses little work.

## Takeaway

The sixteen products split into three onboarding jobs. Recommendation-first apps (Netflix, Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, TikTok, Pinterest) front one taste-signal screen in the first minute, a grid of titles, artists, or interest chips, keep it optional or low-friction, and defer demographics, social graph, and monetization asks to later; Apple Music is the outlier, requiring two such screens (genre, then artist) with no confirmed skip path. Availability-first apps (JustWatch, Reelgood) skip taste entirely and ask only country and which streaming services the user already has, since their job is finding where to watch rather than what to watch. Logging-and-ranking apps (Letterboxd, Criticker, MovieLens, Taste.io, Trakt, Simkl) ask for little or nothing at sign-up and instead gate their payoff (recommendations, scores, compatibility) behind a self-paced minimum of logged or rated items, typically 10 to 20. Beli is the one clear exception to every pattern here: it gates onboarding itself behind a friend-invite wall rather than a taste signal, reported as non-skippable. Likewise sits between the first two groups, combining a short survey with a title-liking grid, and its publisher is the only source in this set with a published completion figure for any onboarding taste question (around 90 percent, per its own ad-sales materials). Company statements explaining the design are thin: only Netflix (jump-start recommendations, plus thumbs replacing stars in 2017 for 200 percent more ratings) and TikTok (feed personalization, from its own newsroom) produced an on-the-record statement this session; Spotify's and Apple's reasoning is available only through teardown paraphrase, not a fetched company page. No app in this set asks a goal or intent question that branches onboarding, and none asks about time available, who the user is watching or listening with, or device context; the one context question anyone asks is location (Beli's city, JustWatch's and Reelgood's country), and it serves content availability or local leaderboards, not scheduling.

## Patterns

1. First minute versus deferred: recommendation-first apps (Netflix, Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, TikTok, Pinterest) ask exactly one kind of taste-signal question in the first minute (titles, artists, or interest chips) and push everything else, demographics, social graph, payment, notification and permission prompts, to after that screen or much later. Availability tools (JustWatch, Reelgood) ask location and owned services instead of taste in that same first minute. Logging apps (Letterboxd, Criticker, MovieLens, Taste.io, Trakt, Simkl) ask almost nothing at sign-up and defer their entire payoff to a self-paced rating or import phase.
2. Favorites versus genres, and how many: specific-item pickers cluster at small numbers, Netflix about 3 titles (company says only "a few," unnumbered), Spotify 3 or more artists, YouTube Music 5 artists, Letterboxd 4 favorite films (a profile feature, not a gate). Category/genre pickers run looser: Pinterest 5 or more interests, TikTok's interest step is optional with no stated minimum, Apple Music runs genre bubbles then artist bubbles with no published count, Trakt and Taste.io ask for genre plus a few titles. No app in this set asks for both a large number of favorites and a genre survey; it is one or the other, kept short.
3. Time, habit, or context questions: almost entirely absent from this category. The only context question anyone asks at onboarding is location, Beli's city (feeding local leaderboards and a per-city density gate already documented in beli_personalization.md) and JustWatch's/Reelgood's country (feeding catalog availability), and neither is about scheduling. No app surveyed asks how much time the user has, when they watch or listen, who with, or what device they use, a contrast with onboarding patterns common in other app categories (fitness, meditation).
4. Goal or intent routing: none found. No app in this set asks a "what are you here for" question that branches the rest of onboarding down a different path; TikTok and the music apps assume one implicit goal (personalize the feed) and skip asking. This is a negative finding, not a confirmed absence industry-wide, since it rests on what this session's sources documented.
5. Imports or account links during onboarding: offered early by Simkl (connect IMDb, Trakt, MyAnimeList, or Letterboxd, or upload a JSON file, positioned as a fast-start path) and, in a soft sense, by JustWatch and Reelgood (the services checklist is a declaration, not an authenticated import) and Criticker (CSV/Letterboxd/IMDb import advertised on its home page). YouTube Music does an implicit, silent import of existing Google/YouTube signal rather than asking. Netflix, Spotify, TikTok, Apple Music, Letterboxd, and Beli offer no import or account-link step at sign-up in the sources found; they all start the taste model cold.
6. Step count and skippability: fewest steps and most skippable are the availability tools (JustWatch, Reelgood) and TikTok, which can be used with zero sign-up at all. Apple Music is the most front-loaded among the majors, two full bubble screens with no confirmed skip. Taste.io is the slowest to a useful result because its "picker" is actually a long rating stream (about 20 items) rather than a single screen. Beli is the only app in this set reported as having a flatly non-skippable onboarding gate (the friend-invite step), which its own 2024 design critique flags as a likely source of early drop-off.
7. Published completion or retention effects: rare in this category. Netflix's 2017 thumbs-for-stars change produced "200% more ratings" (TechCrunch, already cited in comparable_products.md), the clearest consumer-app number found, though it measures a rating-mechanic change, not the title-picker step itself. Likewise's publisher cites completion rates for its own onboarding survey (around 90 percent provide media-preference information, over 80 percent provide demographics), via its ad-sales materials rather than a product blog. The only rigorously published, controlled results on onboarding design in this entire space come from academic MovieLens research, not a consumer product: Rashid et al. (2002) found popularity-based item selection cut new-user rating effort more than threefold versus a naive strategy, and Harper and Konstan (2015) explicitly note they do not know the retention effect of MovieLens v4 dropping its rating gate. No app surveyed published an A/B result for its own current onboarding flow.
8. A silent-import pattern is emerging alongside explicit pickers: YouTube Music (Google/YouTube signal) and, differently, Simkl (explicit but one-click platform connect) both let an existing account substitute for a cold-start picker, while Netflix, Spotify, and Apple Music, despite sitting inside much larger company ecosystems (an existing Apple ID's iTunes purchase history, for instance), do not appear to use that history to pre-seed or skip their own picker screens in the sources found.

## Gaps

- Spotify: no primary Spotify newsroom or help-center page was successfully fetched this session confirming the artist-picker mechanic, its exact minimum or ceiling, or whether it is skippable; all detail comes from teardown blogs and search snippets. The "81% cite personalization" figure's original source and methodology were not confirmed.
- TikTok: the Pageflows screen-by-screen capture used for the signed-up flow does not show an interest-selection screen at all, while other teardowns and TikTok's own newsroom describe interests as central; these may reflect different app versions, regions, or sign-up paths (the Pageflows flow may be the post-2023 account-creation path, with interest selection reserved for the no-account browsing path), but the discrepancy was not reconciled this session. Exact category count and any minimum were not confirmed from a primary source.
- Beli: no Beli company page (help center, blog, or app-store description beyond generic marketing copy) was found describing the onboarding sequence; every finding here is teardown or user report. The 2026-09 Pratt critique did not re-confirm whether the 2024-documented 4-friend invite gate still holds in the current app. Exact screen count and the format of the cuisine-preference step (chip picker versus inferred from behavior) were not confirmed.
- Letterboxd: whether the four-favorite-films prompt appears inside the sign-up flow itself, versus being available only afterward via profile settings, was not confirmed. No onboarding-specific import step was confirmed, though Letterboxd has broader import and export tooling elsewhere.
- Taste.io: the approximately 20-rating figure is an informal, secondary description (Hacker News comment, local-news feature), not a company-published number; taste.io's own About and Press pages were found but not fetched in depth this session.
- MovieLens: current (2026) live-site onboarding was not independently re-verified against the 2015 Harper and Konstan version history; whether movielens.org still uses v4's group-based elicitation or has changed again is unconfirmed.
- Likewise: no screen count, item-count minimum, or skippability was found for its own onboarding flow this session.
- JustWatch and Reelgood: no confirmed numeric minimum or exact screen count beyond general description; whether either shows curated taste-based content (as opposed to pure availability lookup) before or during sign-up was not confirmed.
- Trakt and Simkl: skippability of individual steps (genre selection for Trakt; profile fields for either) was not confirmed; Trakt's screen count is inferred from one teardown's narrative order rather than a numbered, screenshot-backed source.
- Apple Music: skippability of the genre- and artist-bubble screens was not confirmed from any source fetched this session; no published minimum item count was found.
- YouTube Music: whether a Google account with no prior YouTube signal can skip the 5-artist picker was not confirmed. The one company source reached (blog.youtube) described a different, optional, later feature (the 30-artist "tuner") rather than the mandatory onboarding step, so the 5-artist figure remains teardown-sourced only.
- Pinterest: skippability was not confirmed from a fetched source; an Appcues teardown fetch returned only high-level paraphrase rather than a screen-by-screen breakdown. Whether a 2026 flow still matches older (2024 to 2025) documented versions was not independently re-verified.
- General: several primary company pages were reachable only as search-engine snippets rather than full fetches this session (Criticker's get-started page, Letterboxd's welcome page, Spotify's and Pinterest's teardown sources behind what appear to be paywalled or JS-rendered Medium pages), consistent with the fetch limitations already logged in this repo's other research notes. No app in this set, aside from the academic MovieLens literature and Netflix's 2017 ratings-mechanic figure, published a controlled completion or retention result for its onboarding flow, so most retention claims in the Takeaway and Patterns sections above are necessarily silent on causal effect size.
