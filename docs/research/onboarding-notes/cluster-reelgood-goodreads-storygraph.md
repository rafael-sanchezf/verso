# Onboarding flows: Reelgood, Goodreads, The StoryGraph (as of 2026-10-01)

Raw note from a research sub-agent of the first (stalled) inventory run on 2026-10-01, kept because its findings are dated and sourced. Method caveats from the agent: its web search budget ran out partway, web.archive.org and reddit.com could not be fetched at all, and Goodreads' Help Center returned only a loading shell, so Help Center claims rest on search snippets.

## Reelgood

- Flow: (1) download or visit the site, optionally pick region (US or UK); (2) create an account via email or social sign-in, or skip sign-in entirely for the core discovery features; (3) select which streaming services you subscribe to from a checklist; (4) since an August 2024 update, a profile-creation step is part of onboarding; (5) land on the Discover screen, already filtered to the selected services.
- Format: a checkbox-style service picker, tap a plus per logo to add each service. Not a search box, not genre chips, not a swipe deck.
- Minimum items required: none found or stated in any source.
- Skippable: sign-in is explicitly skippable ("that's not necessary to use the Roulette feature", a Skip button described; teardown, How-To Geek, undated). Whether the service step can be skipped with zero services checked was not confirmed either way.
- Imports or account connection: social sign-in (Google, Facebook, Apple per one source; Amazon, Google, Apple per another) at account creation. No evidence of a watchlist import. The service checklist is self-reported, not an account link to the streamers.
- Asked later: service selections are editable any time in settings (teardown, apps.uk, undated). No other deferred prompts found.
- Screens before first useful result: about 2 to 3 (optional sign-in, service checklist, filtered Discover). Confidence medium; assembled from secondary how-to articles.
- Company statement: "Profiles are now part of onboarding for a personalized start" (Reelgood App Store "What's New", v4.8.1, 2024-08-15). No rationale for the service-first order found. No published metrics.
- Sources: company statement, [Reelgood App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/reelgood-streaming-guide/id1031391869), accessed 2026-10-01; teardown, [How to Use Reelgood in 2026, Cloudwards, 2026](https://www.cloudwards.net/how-to-use-reelgood/); teardown, [Reelgood review, Apps UK, undated](https://apps.uk/reelgood-review/); teardown, [How-To Geek, undated](https://www.howtogeek.com/not-sure-what-to-watch-on-your-streaming-services-push-this-button/).
- Gaps: reelgood.com/faq returned 403; no minimum-item figure; service-step skippability unconfirmed; the number of services covered is inconsistent across sources (20 to 150 plus); no company rationale; no Reddit corroboration.

## Goodreads

- Flow: (1) Sign Up with "Continue with Amazon", "Continue with Apple", or email (observed directly on goodreads.com/user/sign_up on 2026-10-01; no Google button appeared on that load, though other sources describe Google as an option); (2) if email, name, email, password; (3) an onboarding step to select favorite genres from a preset list (custom genres can be added) and rate "some" books; (4) the Home page.
- Format: genre selection as tappable items from a preset list plus a free-text custom genre (Help Center content, snippet-sourced). The book-rating step's exact UI (search box versus a pre-populated grid) could not be confirmed.
- Minimum items required: no confirmed blocking minimum at sign-up. The 2024 teardown says users "rate some books" with no number (teardown, Pratt IxD, 2024-09). Separately, Goodreads' 2011 launch post for its recommendation engine says "rate at least 20 books" for recommendations, a statement about when the algorithm produces good output, not a documented sign-up gate; the same post says "rate much more to get even better recommendations". Treat 20 as a historical quality benchmark, not a confirmed current gate.
- Skippable: not confirmed either way.
- Imports: social sign-in only. Goodreads is the service others import from, not an importer.
- Asked later: favorite genres editable any time on the Recommendations page (Help Center, snippet-sourced). An annual reading-goal prompt during onboarding was suggested by search synthesis and is unverified.
- Screens before first useful result: about 3 to 5. Confidence low to medium.
- Company statement: the 2011 post frames the engine as analyzing wide behavioral data with more ratings producing better results. No 2020s statement on the current onboarding design found. No published metrics.
- Sources: company statement, [Announcing Goodreads Personalized Recommendations, Goodreads Blog, 2011-09-15](https://www.goodreads.com/blog/show/303-announcing-goodreads-personalized-recommendations); company statement, [sign-up page](https://www.goodreads.com/user/sign_up), observed 2026-10-01; Help Center (snippet-sourced), [How do I edit genre preferences](https://help.goodreads.com/s/question/0D51H00005SByeTSAT/how-do-i-edit-genre-preferences) and [How can I choose categories for recommendations](https://help.goodreads.com/s/question/0D58V00006ZSCd1SAH/how-can-i-choose-categories-for-recommendations); teardown, [Design Critique: Goodreads, Pratt IxD, 2024-09](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-goodreads-good-books-meet-bad-ux-ios-app/); company statement, [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goodreads-book-tracker-more/id355833469), accessed 2026-10-01.
- Gaps: every Help Center fetch returned a loading shell; the rating UI, the step's skippability, and Google sign-in are unconfirmed; two Medium case studies returned 403; whether 20 ratings is a hard gate anywhere today is unresolved.

## The StoryGraph

- Flow (synthesized across sources; no single end-to-end walkthrough): (1) sign up with email, username, and password, or Google or Apple; (2) email verification; (3) a short post-registration action list including "Get some recommendations" and an offer to import Goodreads data; (4) an explanatory message introducing the Reading Preferences Survey and a "Take the survey" button; (5) the survey: favorite and least-favorite genres, "reference reads" (specific favorite titles), qualities you enjoy, qualities you dislike ("turn-offs"), content warnings to exclude, and an optional Advanced Configuration; (6) recommendations under Find a Book or Recommendations; (7) every answer editable later under Profile, Reading Preferences.
- Format: mixed. Genres list-based (widget unconfirmed). The bulk of the survey is free text, guided by StoryGraph's guidelines to be a comma-separated list (paraphrase of a page that returned 403; not a verified quote). Content warnings are a free-text exclusion list matched against author- and reader-reported warnings. StoryGraph leans on structured free text plus an exclusion list rather than chips or sliders alone.
- Minimum items required: none found. StoryGraph substitutes a stated-preference survey for a ratings-volume gate.
- Skippable: not confirmed; circumstantially optional, since the survey is one of several post-sign-up actions rather than a forced interstitial. A reasoned inference.
- Imports: a Goodreads CSV import, offered shortly after registration ("Import your Goodreads data?") and reachable later under Manage Account (teardown, BookWise, 2026-03-09). That March 2026 guide and a pricing synthesis state the import now requires StoryGraph Plus (4.99 USD a month or 39.99 USD a year), which conflicts with the undated App Store copy describing the import with no paywall. Likely a 2025 to 2026 monetization change; not settled.
- Asked later: the Goodreads import if skipped; Plus upsells (advanced stats, unlimited recommendations, custom charts) during use; Up-Next Suggestions, once Plus-only, made free to all in 2024; Book Clubs (about April 2024) discovered in use.
- Screens before first useful result: about 4 to 6. Confidence low to medium. Sources disagree on whether the survey is one scrolling form or several steps; one multi-section form is the better-supported reading.
- Company statement: the survey guidelines frame the free-text box as a conversation with "a fellow book-lover" to surface the tropes, dynamics, and themes that make someone love a book, rather than genre or rating history alone (corroborated across three secondary sources; primary page not loaded). No survey metrics published. A distinct, gamified Onboarding Reading Challenge reportedly had 22,432 participants in 2024 and 31,558 in 2025 (company pages via search synthesis); not a survey completion rate.
- Sources: company statement, [Revamped Recommendations, StoryGraph Changelog, 2023-12-21](https://roadmap.thestorygraph.com/changelog/revamped-recommendations); company statement, [Disliked Topics and Content Types, StoryGraph Changelog, 2022-11-07](https://roadmap.thestorygraph.com/changelog/disliked-topics-and-content-types-in-recommendations); company statement (blocked, snippet-sourced), [Recommendations Survey Guidelines](https://app.thestorygraph.com/recommendations/guidelines); teardowns, [Molly Christian Tyler, undated](https://mollychristiantyler.com/storygraph-recommendations/), [Reader Voracious, undated](https://readervoracious.com/storygraph-a-better-goodreads-finally/), [This Splendid Shambles, 2026](https://thissplendidshambles.com/the-storygraph-full-review/), [BookWise, 2026-03-09](https://bookwiseapp.com/blog/how-to-import-your-goodreads-library-to-storygraph-step-by-step); company statement, [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1570489264), accessed 2026-10-01.
- Gaps: the guidelines page returned 403 on every attempt; Wayback unreachable; no Reddit access; one form or a wizard unconfirmed; the free-versus-Plus status of the Goodreads import as of 2026-10-01 unresolved.

## Cross-app notes

The three apps are three cold-start strategies. Reelgood's onboarding is a logistics filter (which services you pay for), not taste modeling, and skipping it would undermine the product even if it is not technically blocked. Goodreads gates algorithmic quality behind a volume of behavioral signal (the 2011 "rate at least 20 books"), while its current onboarding asks only a light genre pick plus "some" ratings, which suggests the gate softened into a progressive quality curve. StoryGraph substitutes an explicit stated-preference survey (structured free text plus exclusion lists) for any ratings-volume requirement; its "reference reads" field is kin to asking for named favorites, and it goes further by asking why. A caution rather than a model: StoryGraph appears to have moved its Goodreads import from free to Plus around 2025 to 2026, a live example of a frictionless import at sign-up later monetized.
