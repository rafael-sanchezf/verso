# Hims & Hers: the intake questionnaire, what it did, and the record against it (as of 2026-10-06)

Research date: 2026-10-06. Written for Rafa's question of the same day: he read that Hims & Hers used a sign-up segmentation questionnaire (a "scorecard") that worked very well commercially and later fed FDA, legal, and class-action trouble, and he wants the facts and what Verso's onboarding questionnaire (decision 25, [onboarding-brief.md](../../design/onboarding-brief.md)) should borrow and refuse. Primary sources where reachable: the FTC's complaint, read in full from the redacted PDF filed 2026-07-29; Hims & Hers' shareholder letters on SEC EDGAR; the FDA warning letters; Apple's App Review Guidelines, read 2026-10-06. The rest is trade press and law-firm alerts, each dated. Every complaint and class action below is an allegation, not a finding: none of the 2026 cases has been decided. Items in section 2 carry tags: [questionnaire], [marketing], [billing], [privacy], [compounding], [securities].

## Takeaway

Rafa's premise is about half right. Hims & Hers' sign-up does run through a segmenting questionnaire: pick a condition, answer 5 to 15 questions on interests and goals, create an account, answer 25 to 50 medical questions, see a proposed treatment with its price, then submit. The company credits its "personalized" treatments, which that flow routes people into, with a retention lift of nearly 20 points in the first year. But most of the 2025 to 2026 trouble has nothing to do with the questionnaire. The FDA warning letters, the senators' Super Bowl letter, Novo Nordisk's exit, the securities suits, the SEC inquiry, the DOJ referral, and the patent suit all concern compounded GLP-1 drugs and how they were advertised. The questionnaire is central to one strand: the FTC's July 2026 suit with California and Utah, and the privacy class actions that followed it. Even there, nobody alleges that segmenting people or asking them questions was wrong. The allegations are about three things. Pressing submit silently authorized a charge and a subscription. The copy promised a "free consult" to find what is "right for you", and most people never got one. And the answers went to Meta, Snap, and a dozen other advertising pixels. One word runs through both strands: "personalized". Novo called it a "false guise" for mass compounding, and the FTC alleges that the personal consultation it implied did not happen. For Verso, keep the questionnaire's mechanics: one routing question, a short cheap block first, and a result built visibly from the answers and shown early. Refuse what made Hims & Hers' version actionable: claims the flow cannot back, consent that rides on another button, answers that leave the app, and exits that are harder than entrances.

## 1. The product mechanic

### Documented

- The flow, as the FTC describes it (complaint ¶19, filed 2026-07-29, N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-07871, [PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)): every prescription is sold as a subscription and starts with an online "Intake Flow" in four parts. (1) About 5 to 15 questions on "treatment interests and goals". (2) Account creation. (3) A medical assessment of 25 to 50 questions. (4) Screens showing "a potential treatment that a medical provider may prescribe, along with the price and frequency of the refill shipments", then billing and shipping details, then a submit button that sends the flow to a provider.
- Segmentation happens at the door: a visitor "selects the type of medical treatment that they are interested in" on the homepage, and that choice starts the condition-specific Intake Flow (complaint ¶25, [PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). Hims & Hers' own description says the intake form is dynamic, changing with the answers, and that a provider licensed in the patient's state reviews it, asynchronously, with unlimited messaging and "over 20 million consultations" since founding (company statement, [Hims & Hers newsroom, 2024-07-08](https://news.hims.com/newsroom/how-hims-hers-is-increasing-access-to-care-for-everyone)).
- The framing around the questionnaire was "free" and "for you". Since at least 2019, ads have called the consultations free (¶20), often offering a "free consult" (¶21). From 2021 to 2023 the homepage offered "free online visit[s]", and from early 2023 to early 2024 it said "Your free online visit starts here". Inside the flow the copy read "Free provider diagnosis: A licensed medical provider will assess what you shared today to determine if treatment is right for you" and promised a "customized care plan that's right for you". The final button read "Due Now - $0" and "Pay $0 today", over the line "You will only be charged if prescribed" (complaint ¶20 to ¶26, [PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)).
- What "personalized" means in the product: "a non-commercially available dosage to balance efficacy and side-effects", "an alternative form factor to support adherence", or "a single solution treating multiple conditions" (company statement, [Q2 2025 shareholder letter, 2025-08-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375125000248/finalq22025shareholderle.htm)). The questionnaire is the routing step, and the personalized product is where it routes people.

### Evidence it worked (all company statements, none independently audited)

- 60% of subscribers were on a personalized treatment at the end of Q1 2025, and over 80% in dermatology. Personalization "lifted retention nearly 20 points for subscribers in their first year" over two years and helped hold hair-loss subscriber growth above 45% for men and 170% for women ([Q1 2025 letter, 2025-05-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375125000152/finalq12025shareholderle.htm)).
- "Nearly 70% of the new subscribers who joined the platform in the quarter" used a personalized plan. Subscriber growth was "primarily driven by increased traffic ... as a result of our marketing activities and improved onsite and customer onboarding experiences, as well as robust adoption of personalized treatment plans" ([Q2 2025 letter, 2025-08-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375125000248/finalq22025shareholderle.htm)).
- More than 2.5 million subscribers and about 2.35 billion USD of revenue in 2025, up 59% ([FY2025 results, 2026-02-23](https://investors.hims.com/news/news-details/2026/Hims--Hers-Health-Inc--Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx)).

### Asserted, not established

- No public number isolates the questionnaire. The retention claim belongs to the personalized products, and the onboarding claim is one clause beside marketing spend. No A/B test, completion rate, or conversion rate for the intake flow was found.
- "Scorecard": no source found that uses the word for Hims & Hers' intake. Unverified.
- Teardown details such as a progress bar, a "Reviewing your answers" interstitial, or a "you're eligible" reveal turned up only in search summaries of AI-generated teardown pages, so they are unverified. Lazyweb lists a 10-step Hims onboarding flow updated May 2026, but shows only 5 screens without a sign-up ([Lazyweb, updated May 2026](https://lazyweb.com/canvas/flows/hims/onboarding)).

## 2. The trouble, 2025 to 2026

- **Senators' letter on the Super Bowl ad** [marketing, compounding]. Senators Durbin and Marshall wrote to the FDA on 2025-02-07. They said the ad for compounded GLP-1s had no side-effect or risk information and flashed "not FDA-approved" for three seconds in small type. The letter does not mention the questionnaire or prescribing practices ([Durbin release, 2025-02-07](https://www.durbin.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/durbin-marshall-draw-fda-attention-to-misleading-drug-commercial-set-to-run-during-super-bowl); [Healthcare Brew, 2025-02-07](https://www.healthcare-brew.com/stories/2025/02/07/lawmakers-health-experts-fda-pull-hims-hers-super-bowl-ad)). No FDA action on the ad itself was found.
- **Novo Nordisk partnership, 2025-04-29 to 2025-06-23** [compounding, marketing, "personalization"]. Novo ended the Wegovy collaboration after about eight weeks. It said Hims & Hers broke the law, which "prohibits mass sales of compounded drugs under the false guise of 'personalization'", and was spreading deceptive marketing. The shares fell 34.6% that day ([UPI, 2025-06-23](https://www.upi.com/Health_News/2025/06/23/novo-nordisk-ends-partnership-with-himshers/1441750690952/); [STAT, 2025-06-23](https://www.statnews.com/2025/06/23/novo-nordisk-ends-deal-with-hims-due-compounding-concerns-obesity-drugs-glp1/)). The target is the personalized product the questionnaire hands out, not the questionnaire.
- **Securities class actions** [securities]. *Sookdeo v. Hims & Hers* (N.D. Cal. No. 3:25-cv-05315) and *Yaghsizian* (No. 3:25-cv-05321) were filed 2025-06-25 for buyers from 2025-04-29 to 2025-06-22 or 23 (the filings differ). They allege the company hid its "deceptive promotion" of knockoff Wegovy and the risk that Novo would walk ([Hagens Berman via Newsfile, 2025-07-31](https://www2.newsfilecorp.com/release/260903/Hims-Hers-Health-Inc.-HIMS-Faces-Securities-Class-Actions-After-Novo-Nordisk-Cancels-Partnership-Over-Deceptive-Marketing-of-WegovyR-Hagens-Berman)). Status: the defendants moved to dismiss the amended complaint on 2026-03-19, and the motion was being briefed ([Kessler Topaz case page, read 2026-10-06](https://www.ktmc.com/?p=10071)).
- **FTC investigation reported** [billing, marketing]. Bloomberg reported on 2025-08-14 that the FTC had been looking at complaints about Hims & Hers' ads and cancellation for over a year ([Bloomberg Law, 2025-08-14](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/ftc-probing-complaints-about-hims-ads-cancellation-practices); [Seeking Alpha, 2025-08-14](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4485799-hims-and-hers-health-under-investigation-ftc-business-practices)). I could not open the article and read it through search summaries.
- **FDA warning letters** [marketing, compounding]. On 2025-09-09 the FDA wrote to Hims and to Hers. It cited the website claims "Same active ingredient as Ozempic and Wegovy" and "Clinically proven ingredients" as false or misleading, making the compounded products misbranded under FDCA sections 502(a) and 502(bb). The letters do not mention the intake questionnaire, personalization, or prescribing ([Hims letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/hims-hers-health-inc-dba-hims-716567-09092025); [Hers letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/hims-hers-health-inc-dba-hers-716825-09092025)). They were among more than 100 letters posted that week to drugmakers and online prescribers, after a presidential memo ordered drug ads made "truthful and non-misleading" ([Fortune, 2025-09-16](https://fortune.com/2025/09/16/fda-weight-loss-drugmakers-false-misleading-advertising-telehealth)).
- **The compounded semaglutide pill, February 2026** [compounding]. Hims & Hers announced a compounded copy of the Wegovy pill at 49 USD for the first month and 99 USD after ([Pharmaceutical Technology, 2026-02-06](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/news/fda-targets-illegal-drug-copies-amid-latest-hims-hers-novo-nordisk-flashpoint/)). On 2026-02-06 the FDA said it would restrict the ingredients used in compounded GLP-1s, and the HHS general counsel referred Hims & Hers to the Department of Justice ([STAT, 2026-02-06](https://www.statnews.com/pharmalot/2026/02/06/fda-take-action-against-hims-telehealth-wegovy-compounding)). Hims & Hers withdrew the pill the next day, and Novo filed a patent suit on 2026-02-09 ([Healthcare Dive, February 2026](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/hims-stop-launch-compounded-wegovy-fda-novo/811678)). They settled on 2026-03-09: Hims & Hers stops selling compounded copies of Novo's drugs and sells Wegovy and Ozempic, and Novo may refile ([NJBIZ, 2026-03-09](https://njbiz.com/novo-nordisk-hims-hers-glp1-drug-settlement/)). No outcome of the DOJ referral was found.
- **SEC investigation** [securities]. The SEC's Division of Enforcement asked Hims & Hers to preserve documents on its public statements about compounded semaglutide. The company disclosed this in its annual report in February 2026 ([Börse Express, 2026-02-24](https://www.boerse-express.com/news/articles/hims-hers-aktie-sec-ermittelt-869278)).
- **FTC, California, and Utah v. Hims & Hers, filed 2026-07-29** [questionnaire, billing, privacy, marketing]. This is the one action built on the questionnaire. The complaint was authorized by a 2-0 Commission vote; California is represented by Los Angeles County Counsel ([FTC release, 2026-07-29](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-states-act-against-hims-hers-deceptive-unlawful-privacy-practices)). The allegations, by paragraph ([complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)):
  - **Who and the core claim (¶4).** "Hundreds of thousands" of consumers were misled into believing that submitting the intake form did not commit them to buy.
  - **No consultation, charge first (¶27).** Most consumers got no consultation, live or asynchronous. Providers "unilaterally prescribe", and the company charges and enrolls the consumer in a subscription before they "even learn what treatment the provider has recommended". Footnote 1 ties this to states that do not require a live visit.
  - **The buried disclosure (¶29 to ¶30).** The line "if prescribed medication, you are purchasing an automatically-renewing subscription" sat "in small, low-contrast font below the final call-to-action button".
  - **The cancel path (¶45 to ¶55).** Before April 2023 most consumers could cancel only by phone, email, or chat. Afterwards, cancelling meant pressing "Add/remove items from order" and then three to ten survey screens. Single-click cancel had existed in California and Colorado since June 2022, but other states got the version "refined to successfully obstruct cancellations".
  - **Answers sent to advertisers (¶67 to ¶77).** Health information went to Meta and Snap through customer lists and tracking events. Pixels from Microsoft, Google, Criteo, Pinterest, Reddit, TikTok, the Trade Desk, X, and others sat on the site. The event details are redacted in the public version.
  - **Counts and status.** The counts are FTC Act section 5, ROSCA, California's False Advertising and Unfair Competition laws including the Automatic Renewal Law, and Utah's Consumer Sales Practices Act ([McDermott Will & Schulte, 2026-08-24](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/ftc-two-states-take-aim-at-hims-hers-subscription-and-health-data-practices/)). Hims & Hers says the FTC "contorts the law to try to manufacture claims" ([NPR via VPM, 2026-07-30](https://www.vpm.org/npr-news/npr-news/2026-07-30/the-federal-trade-commission-is-suing-the-popular-telehealth-company-hims-hers)). The case is pending; the unredacted complaint was posted 2026-08-31 ([FTC case page, read 2026-10-06](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/hims-hers)).
- **Consumer class actions that name the intake form** [questionnaire, privacy, billing]. All three are early stage.
  - *Doe v. Hims & Hers* (N.D. Cal., filed 2026-07-30) alleges that answers to the roughly 35-question testosterone questionnaire went to Meta, Google, Snap, Amplitude, and others. It says Google received full page addresses showing which questionnaire was open, and quotes the privacy policy: "We do not use protected information for advertising or marketing" ([ClaimDepot, undated case page, read 2026-10-06](https://www.claimdepot.com/cases/hims-hers-lawsuit-claims-company-secretly-shared-patients-health-data-with-meta-snap-and-others)).
  - *Pearson v. Hims & Hers* (No. 4:26-cv-08470, filed 2026-08-14) adds California Automatic Renewal Law claims about charging and enrolling people right after the intake form ([ClassAction.org, 2026-09-02](https://www.classaction.org/news/hims-and-hers-lawsuit-claims-users-health-info-is-shared-with-third-parties-without-consent)).
  - *Holbrook v. Hims & Hers* (M.D. Fla. No. 3:26-cv-02286, filed 2026-09-01) alleges that treatment goals, symptoms, mood, and family history went to nine firms ([Top Class Actions, 2026-09-11](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/hims-hers-health-faces-class-action-over-alleged-website-tracking/)).
- **Context: questionnaire-only prescribing across the industry** [questionnaire]. A Yale secret-shopper study of 49 GLP-1 sites, run August to December 2025, got prescriptions from 45. It found 26.5% required a video visit, some approvals came in five minutes or less, and 75.6% charged and shipped without asking for confirmation (Chetty et al., *JAMA* 2026, [doi:10.1001/jama.2026.9131](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.9131); [Healio, 2026-07-09](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20260709/secret-shopper-study-most-websites-prescribe-glp1s-without-clinician-visit)). The coverage names no company. No FDA action aimed at questionnaire-based prescribing itself was found.

### Where the premise holds and where it does not

- **Holds.** The FTC case and three consumer suits are about the intake questionnaire. The complaint quotes users ("After completing the questionnaire, I was automatically enrolled ...", ¶33), and it says Hims & Hers kept the copy despite "years of receiving complaints" (¶5) ([complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)).
- **Does not hold.** The FDA letters, the senators' letter, Novo's exit, the securities cases, the SEC inquiry, the DOJ referral, and the patent suit are about compounding and advertising claims. None names the questionnaire.
- **What was wrong in the questionnaire cases.** Not segmentation or asking questions. Four things: what submitting silently authorized, copy that promised a consultation that did not happen, a disclosure placed under the button, and answers that reached advertisers. The common thread with the compounding strand is a claim of personal treatment ("personalized", "right for you") that the process did not deliver.

## 3. What is transferable: what Verso borrows, what Verso refuses

Verso is not medical, and no health-data law applies to it. What applies is Apple's App Review Guidelines (5.1.1, 5.1.2, 3.1.2, 2.3.1, [read 2026-10-06](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), the FTC Act and ROSCA, and California's Automatic Renewal Law. The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 2025-07-08 ([Greenberg Traurig, 2025-07](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/7/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)), but ROSCA still carried the Amazon Prime settlement of 2.5 billion USD on 2025-09-25 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/amazoncom-inc-rosca-ftc-v-timeline-item-2025-09-25)) and three counts of the Hims & Hers complaint. Verso's flow (decision 25): which media, where you play or watch, three titles you love, a page of popular titles, a comparison session, a permission step before a model writes reasons, then the first pick.

### What Verso borrows

1. **One routing question, then a branch per answer.** Hims & Hers segments at the door and runs a condition-specific, dynamic form ([complaint ¶25](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf); [newsroom, 2024-07-08](https://news.hims.com/newsroom/how-hims-hers-is-increasing-access-to-care-for-everyone)). Verso's media picker already does this. Keep it the only question before the per-medium branch.
2. **The cheap, goal-shaped block before the heavy one.** Hims & Hers asks 5 to 15 questions about interests and goals before the account, and 25 to 50 medical questions after it ([complaint ¶19](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). Verso's equivalent: media and platforms are cheap, and the three titles and the deck are the work. Inference with no published test behind it: the media question could come before sign-up, since Verso's runs after it (ROADMAP item on the questionnaire). That is a question for the frames, not a decision.
3. **Show what the answers produced before asking for anything else.** The fourth part of Hims & Hers' flow shows the proposed treatment and its price before billing ([complaint ¶19](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). Verso's version is the first ranked list and the first pick with its reason, before any paywall, notification prompt, or friends step. The day-0 evidence is in [preference_elicitation.md](preference-elicitation.md), guidance 10.
4. **Make "personalized" a property of the output.** The retention lift Hims & Hers claims attaches to products that really differ per person: dose, form, conditions combined ([Q1 2025 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375125000152/finalq12025shareholderle.htm); [Q2 2025 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375125000248/finalq22025shareholderle.htm)). It is company-asserted. Verso's version is a reason that cites the user's own titles (decision 24), which a user can check.

### What Verso refuses

1. **Copy the flow cannot back.** Hims & Hers promised a "free consult" to find what is "right for you", and most users got none ([complaint ¶4, ¶24 to ¶27](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). Novo called the personalization a "false guise" ([UPI, 2025-06-23](https://www.upi.com/Health_News/2025/06/23/novo-nordisk-ends-partnership-with-himshers/1441750690952/)). The FDA objected to wording that implied sameness ([warning letter, 2025-09-09](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/hims-hers-health-inc-dba-hims-716567-09092025)). Apple 2.3.1(a) bars "promoting content or services that it does not actually offer". Verso: no "personalized", "for you", or "knows your taste" as sales words on the questionnaire or the paywall. Each pick names its source ("popular on your platforms", "close to X and Y in your Loved tier"). No copy implies a neighbor prediction before the harness clears it (decision 24).
2. **Consent that rides on another button.** At Hims & Hers, "Pay $0 today" authorized a future charge and a renewing subscription, and the disclosure sat under the button in small, low-contrast type ([complaint ¶26, ¶29 to ¶30](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). California has required express affirmative consent to renewal terms since 2025-07-01 ([Barnes & Thornburg, 2025](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect)). Apple 3.1.2(c) requires saying what the user gets for the price before subscribing, and 3.1.2(a) removes apps that "trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses". Verso: no questionnaire or deck answer ever starts a trial or a subscription. The trial is its own screen, with the price, the renewal date, and how to cancel above the button.
3. **Data consent buried in the flow, or with no way to say no.** Apple 5.1.2(i), clarified 2025-11-13 ([Apple Developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ey6d8onl)), requires disclosing sharing "with third-party AI" and getting explicit permission first. Apple 5.1.1(ii) says "paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data". Apple 5.1.1(iv) asks for alternatives when users decline. The FTC's 2024 sweep found "sneaking" and "interface interference" the most common dark patterns, and looked for designs that get people to share more than they meant to ([FTC, 2024-07-10](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-icpen-gpen-announce-results-review-use-dark-patterns-affecting-subscription-services-privacy)). Verso: the permission step is its own screen. It names what is sent (titles, tiers, notes) and to whom, and its two buttons carry equal weight. A "No" still returns three picks through the `suggest` function's SQL fallback (#63), for paid users too. Withdrawal is one toggle in settings. Whether Apple reads 5.1.1(ii) as covering a model call is my inference, not Apple's stated position.
4. **Answers that leave the app.** Hims & Hers allegedly shared customer lists and tracking events with Meta and Snap, and ran pixels from at least thirteen more ad platforms ([complaint ¶67 to ¶77](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). Page addresses alone told Google which questionnaire a user had open ([ClaimDepot on *Doe*, read 2026-10-06](https://www.claimdepot.com/cases/hims-hers-lawsuit-claims-company-secretly-shared-patients-health-data-with-meta-snap-and-others)). Two FTC precedents point the same way. BetterHelp used the questionnaire fact "that they had previously been in therapy" to target lookalike ads ([FTC, 2023-03-02](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/03/ftc-ban-betterhelp-revealing-consumers-data-including-sensitive-mental-health-information-facebook)), and Cerebral was banned from using health information for most advertising ([FTC, 2024-04-15](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/proposed-ftc-order-will-prohibit-telehealth-firm-cerebral-using-or-disclosing-sensitive-data)). Apple 5.1.2(ii) bars repurposing data without fresh consent. Verso already rules out third-party analytics SDKs and keeps funnel events in its own table (ROADMAP, wave A). Extend that to the later public web site, which gets no ad pixels, and keep questionnaire answers out of URLs and event names.
5. **Questions with no consumer.** Apple 5.1.1(iii) says to "only collect and use data that is required to accomplish the relevant task". At Hims & Hers, the collected answers found a second use in advertising ([complaint ¶67](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). Verso: each question needs a use in the same session. Media chooses the decks, platforms filter the deck, and the three titles seed the comparisons. No demographics, mood, or goal questions, as the onboarding brief already decided.
6. **Exits harder than entrances.** At Hims & Hers, the cancel path ran through "Add/remove items from order" and three to ten survey screens. One-click cancel existed only where California and Colorado law forced it ([complaint ¶45 to ¶55](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). Cerebral removed easy cancellation after two weeks because cancellations rose ([FTC, 2024-04-15](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/proposed-ftc-order-will-prohibit-telehealth-firm-cerebral-using-or-disclosing-sensitive-data)). Verso: "Manage subscription" opens the system's subscription screen directly, with no survey wall in front. The flow is the same in every state and country.
7. **Deciding for the user and telling them later.** Hims & Hers' providers "unilaterally prescribe", and users learn the treatment after the charge ([complaint ¶27](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Hims-Complaint-Redacted-E-Filed.pdf)). In the JAMA study, 75.6% of sites shipped without confirmation ([Healio, 2026-07-09](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20260709/secret-shopper-study-most-websites-prescribe-glp1s-without-clinician-visit)). Verso's analog is quieter but real. Provisional placements from the batch session (decision 27) are shown as provisional on the review and fixable there. The deck never marks a title played or watched without a tap (rule 35).
