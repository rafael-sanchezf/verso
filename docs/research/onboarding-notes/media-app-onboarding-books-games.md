# Onboarding inventory: book and game trackers, plus goal/quiz-style reference apps

State as of 2026-10-01. Every claim below is dated to its source and labeled: "company" (the app's own blog, help page, changelog, or app-store listing), "teardown" (a UX/app-analytics site or design critique that walked the flow), or "user report" (a review, forum post, or search-engine synthesis of reviews, not independently screenshotted by this note). Research done directly with WebSearch and WebFetch, no sub-agents, no live walkthroughs or screenshots taken.

## Takeaway

Across the fourteen apps and reference points checked, two onboarding philosophies dominate. Taste-first apps open with an explicit preference step before anything else is useful: Goodreads (genre chips, then ratings, company statement dated 2011-09-15), StoryGraph (a full Reading Preferences Survey), Duolingo (a motivation and goal question, then an adaptive placement test), Headspace (a goals/experience quiz), and Stitch Fix (a roughly 15-minute style quiz built around a 24-image "style sphere" question documented in a 2016-11-30 engineering post) all ask before they deliver anything. Import-first and utility-first apps instead treat onboarding as plumbing: Hardcover puts a Goodreads, StoryGraph, or CSV import inside its onboarding sequence ahead of any taste question, the Xbox and PlayStation apps ask nothing about content taste because their job is signing into an existing account and pairing an existing console, and Steam's Interactive Recommender has no onboarding at all, it is a voluntary store page that reads silently off playtime history and, by Valve's own 2019 statement, cannot help a brand-new account with no play history. Game trackers built from scratch (SavePoint, Backloggd, GG) sit in between: none was found to ask a taste question at sign-up, each defers personalization to a specific feature used later (SavePoint's Decision Engine takes mood, time, and platform only when picking tonight's game), and import is either absent, unconfirmed, or paywalled (SavePoint gates Steam, Xbox, and PSN sync to its $9/month Pro tier while leaving BoardGameGeek import free). The newest AI-native entrants break the pattern further: Achriom, checked first per the brief, now advertises starting from an imported library instead of a blank questionnaire ("you start with your full collection, not a blank page," company site, fetched 2026-10-01), and TasteRay replaces a ratings gate with a single free-text mood box; neither opens with a classic multi-step questionnaire, and no clean 2024-2026 example of one was found for books, games, or cross-media. The only apps with published, numbered evidence that an onboarding choice moves a real metric are Duolingo (several dated A/B tests on its own blog, with retention lifts from about 1% up to +14% depending on the test) and, through a vendor case study with a disclosed commercial interest, Headspace (a quiz variant roughly doubling trial-to-course-start conversion in an April 2026 experiment, with no significant effect on actual meditation days in the same writeup).

## Patterns

1. What the best apps ask in the first minute, and what they defer: Duolingo and Headspace ask a goal/motivation question immediately, before any account exists in Duolingo's case, and defer account creation itself to after a first taste of value. Hardcover and StoryGraph ask for an import or a full survey immediately and defer nothing, because migration quality or recommendation quality is the whole pitch. Steam, Xbox, and PlayStation defer everything content-related indefinitely, because these apps are utilities (store, console pairing, account management), not discovery products, so there is nothing to ask.
2. Favorites (specific titles) versus genres and moods, and how many: genres and moods dominate the first screen (Goodreads' genre chips; StoryGraph's genre-plus-mood survey; Headspace's goal categories). Specific-title picks appear as a second step once genre narrows the field (Goodreads moves to rating specific books surfaced from the chosen genres). Hardcover substitutes an import of hundreds of specific titles for hand-picking a few. Counts range from StoryGraph's open-ended, uncounted survey down to Duolingo's single motivation choice and TasteRay's single free-text box; Stitch Fix is the outlier with a quantified 24-image question alone, inside a roughly 15-minute total quiz (company source, 2016-11-30).
3. Time, habit, or context questions: Duolingo (a daily time goal, 5/10/15 minutes), Headspace (available time, in the quiz variants tested in the 2026 experiment), Stitch Fix (how much time/effort the user puts into style, a habit proxy), and SavePoint's Decision Engine (time available plus platform) all ask about time or context, but only Duolingo and Headspace ask it at account-level onboarding; SavePoint asks it at the point of use, not sign-up. No book app or pure social game tracker in this set (Goodreads, StoryGraph, Hardcover, Fable, Backloggd, GG) asks a time or habit question at onboarding.
4. Goal or intent questions that route content, and their known effect: Duolingo ("why are you learning") and Headspace (meditation goal) both route subsequent content on the answer, and both have documented evidence it works. Duolingo's own blog (2017-05-10, re-surfaced in current retention documentation) reports a goal/streak-separation test lifting Day-14 retention by 3.3%, daily active learners by 1%, and the share of daily learners holding a streak by 10.5% overall and 19% among new learners within 20 days; a separate Streak Wager test lifted Day-7 retention by 14%. Headspace's quiz-plus-course-match variant roughly doubled trial-to-course-start conversion (31% to 63%) in an April 2026 experiment described by vendor Purchasely, though the same writeup found no statistically significant lift in actual active meditation days, a useful caution that a completion-rate win is not automatically a behavior-change win.
5. Imports offered during onboarding versus deferred: offered immediately, inside onboarding: Hardcover (Goodreads, StoryGraph, CSV), StoryGraph (Goodreads), Achriom (Goodreads, Letterboxd, Trakt, Apple Music, AniList, or a shelf photo). Offered later or gated: SavePoint (BoardGameGeek free; Steam, Xbox, PlayStation Network behind a $9/month or $99/year Pro paywall). Not found at all: Goodreads itself, Fable, Backloggd, GG, Steam, Xbox, PlayStation, TasteRay. The pattern holds across media: import-at-onboarding correlates with "we are the destination for people leaving a specific named incumbent," not with category (books versus games) as such.
6. Step counts, and whether everything is skippable: counts exist only where a source gave one: Fable (13 onboarding screens, app-analytics teardown), Hardcover (about 5 to 6), Duolingo (about 5 to 6 question screens before a first lesson, with account creation pushed later still), Goodreads (about 3). Full-flow skippability was confirmed for individual steps only (Hardcover's final personal-details screen; Duolingo's deferred sign-up; Steam, Xbox, and PlayStation's taste questions, which do not exist to skip) rather than for entire flows; most sources were silent on skippability, which is marked "not confirmed" per app in the table rather than assumed.
7. Published completion or retention results: Duolingo has by far the clearest trail, several dated, numbered experiments on its own blog. Headspace's evidence comes through a vendor case study with specific percentages but a disclosed commercial interest in the outcome. No book or game tracker in this set (Goodreads, StoryGraph, Hardcover, Fable, SavePoint, Backloggd, GG) publishes onboarding-specific completion or retention numbers in anything found this session; their minimum-rating gates (Goodreads' 20, and Criticker's 10 for comparison, per comparable_products.md) are asserted as thresholds, not backed by published before/after data.
8. The AI-native wildcard search itself is a pattern: the two 2024-2026 candidates actually confirmed (Achriom, TasteRay) both abandon the questionnaire in favor of import-first or single-free-text-first onboarding, explicitly marketing that abandonment as a selling point against the older rating-gate model (Goodreads, Criticker, the pre-2024 "Taste" app).

## Gaps

- No app in this note was walked live in a browser or screenshotted; every step order is reconstructed from teardown sites, search-engine synthesis of reviews, official changelogs, help pages, and app-store listings, which can go stale, disagree with each other, or in a few cases this session turn out to be search-engine noise rather than real sources.
- Backloggd's own changelog page could not be fetched this session (connection refused, on two attempts). A search result describing a "Steam step in onboarding" for Backloggd traced back to an unrelated GitHub side project (samuelaredman/astro-supabase-sam-bill, not Backloggd's own code) and was discarded; whether Backloggd offers any account-linking step during onboarding remains unconfirmed either way.
- A separate search for Backloggd's Discovery Engine onboarding surfaced two more unrelated GitHub repositories (brookstalley/prawduct and AliMoradpourArani/indie-game-platform) whose commit messages happened to match the search terms; also discarded. This pattern, a search engine blending an unrelated open-source project's text into a product-research answer, recurred enough this session that any search-synthesized claim not traceable to a single named, checkable article was treated as low-confidence and flagged as such in the table rather than stated plainly.
- GG's version history was confirmed only up to v1.10.0 (July 2024, via its App Store listing). No 2025 or 2026 version notes were found, so "no restrictive onboarding as of October 2026" is an inference from the last confirmed state, not a directly observed current fact.
- SavePoint's actual account-creation screen, as distinct from its Decision Engine mechanic, which is well documented in its own FAQ, was not found in any source.
- Fable's onboarding screens are blurred behind a third-party teardown tool's own paywall; only the screen count (13) and general topic areas (goals, currently-reading books) were recovered, not the specific questions, formats, or skippability.
- The most promising PlayStation App teardown found (medium.com/design-bootcamp, "PlayStation App: Better words, better sign-up flow") returned HTTP 403 and could not be read; only its title and the fact that it exists are confirmed.
- The Xbox "under two minutes," "notification permissions" description came from an uncredited search-engine synthesis rather than a single named, checkable page, and is flagged low-confidence in the table.
- No 2024-2026 AI recommendation app for books, games, or cross-media was found that opens with a classic multi-step questionnaire the way Stitch Fix, Goodreads, or the pre-2024 "Taste" app do. Achriom (checked first, per instructions) is import-first. TasteRay is a single free-text mood box and covers movies only today. "Taste" itself matches the questionnaire-plus-rating-gate pattern well (genre and favorite-film picks, then swipe-style four-point ratings on roughly 20 titles before suggestions start) but predates the window (launched 2016; "Taste 2.0" on Product Hunt 2023-10-16). BookRecommendation.ai advertises "five quick questions" for book recommendations, but the page fetched this session was a thin landing page rather than the quiz itself, so its exact questions, order, format, skippability, company, and launch date are all unconfirmed. This is reported as a substantive finding (the newest entrants may be moving away from questionnaires entirely), not as a failed search.
- Stitch Fix's style-quiz detail comes from a 2016-11-30 engineering blog post about the men's-service beta; it is not confirmed whether the 24-image "style sphere" question or its yes/no format is still in the 2026 production quiz, only that it was designed this way at launch. The total "about 15 minutes" duration is a user-report aggregate, not a company figure.
- No minimum-rating-style gate (comparable to Goodreads' 20 or Criticker's 10) was found published for StoryGraph, Hardcover, Fable, SavePoint, Backloggd, or GG; absence of a published number is recorded as a gap, not as evidence that no gate exists.
