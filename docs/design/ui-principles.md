# UI principles

A running file. Everything Rafa has said about how the interface should feel and behave, plus the class principles it leans on. Update it whenever a rule is added or changed, with the date. Decisions about specific screens live in [ui-brief.md](ui-brief.md); this file is the why and the rules.

## Sources

- Krug, *Don't Make Me Think, Revisited* (UI/UX class reading): scan, don't read; satisfice; muddle through; conventions; visual hierarchy; mindless clicks; noise reduction; trunk test; test with three people.
- Two design courses the developer took, UI/UX and human-centered design. "Rule of three" is Rafa's stated principle from the human-centered design course; treat it as a chunking rule: three things per screen, three items per block, three variants per decision.
- Beli, the product whose mechanic and social layer we are adapting. Its UI is one of the options on every screen, applied to games.
- Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, since the app is iOS-first. Since 2026-09-29 the HIG text is on this machine at `~/.claude/skills/apple-design/references/hig/` (one Markdown file per page, with Apple's change dates; `hig-lookup.md` is the index). Cite a page and heading (`tab-bars.md › Best practices`) rather than the HIG from memory, and read the file before citing it. Reviews against it are filed in [reviews/](../private/design/reviews/); how and when they run is in [figma-workflow.md](figma-workflow.md), working loop steps 4 and 6.

## The product in one line

Rank what you play by comparing two things at a time, keep what's next in one place, and eventually see what friends think. The interface serves that loop; everything else is context.

## Rules

1. **Intentional, not incidental.** Every screen is designed in Figma and reviewed before it is coded. No screen ships as whatever the first implementation produced.
2. **Structure before direction before fidelity.** React to grayscale layouts first, then to a visual direction on the two screens that matter most, then apply it everywhere. Never ask someone to judge layout and color in the same round.
3. **Rule of three.** Three blocks on Home. Three items in a block before "see all." Two or three variants per decision, never one.
4. **One medium at a time, hard walls.** The app is always in exactly one medium. Each medium has a color, an icon, and possibly its own tab set and home structure. Cross-medium features are deliberately minimal.
5. **Color is the brand's, never the medium's.** One brand green marks the action and the active state; the medium is said by its icon, its word and its content. Every colored surface is a deep solid with white on it, the same value in light and dark; brand text and icons are the one thing that lifts in dark mode. (Rewritten 2026-10-07 from decision 29, decided on the Foundations boards; it read "Color is a signal, never the only signal" with games green, books orange, movies blue, TV purple from 2026-09-15 to 2026-10-07.)
6. **Tap, don't swipe, to change modes.** Switching media is a tap on a visible icon. Horizontal swipes are reserved for lists inside a page and for iOS back.
7. **Conventions first.** iOS patterns (large titles that collapse, tab bars, sheets, segmented controls) unless there is a written reason to deviate. Deviations get a note in the brief.
8. **Labels match.** A tab and its page share a name, or the page name is an obvious expansion of the tab. Headers are short; a large title should not wrap.
9. **Show what you know, say what you don't.** When data is missing (hours played, release date), fall back down a defined hierarchy rather than showing an empty field, and never fake a number.
10. **The core loop is never more than one tap away.** From any game, "I played this" starts the tier and comparison flow. From the backlog or wishlist, "I'm starting this" is one tap.
11. **Order belongs to the user.** Lists that represent intent (backlog, up next) are user-ordered by drag, with the app's sort as a starting point only.
12. **Escape hatches, quietly.** Every forced choice has a way out ("too close," "can't compare"), styled as secondary so it is available but not tempting.
13. **Confirm the one careless tap.** Anything that silently changes a ranking shows the result in context and lets the user nudge it before it sticks.
14. **Empty states are screens.** Signed out, first run, and empty blocks are designed with the same care as full states.
15. **Sharing is a feature of the ranking.** The ranked list and the collection view should look good enough to screenshot and share without a special mode; a dedicated share image comes later.
16. **Cut words.** Krug's rule: halve the copy, then halve it again. One prompt for comparisons, fixed wording, printed on the screen.
17. **Test with three people.**
18. **Per-medium vocabulary.** "Backlog" and "Add to backlog" for games, because that is the word the space uses. "Want to watch" for movies and TV, "Want to read" for books. The same list, named the way each audience names it. (2026-09-15)
19. **Ownership is its own fact.** Having a game is separate from wanting to play it or having played it. You can own a gift you will never start; you can want a game you don't own. Ownership carries the platforms you have it on and is set from the game page, the add-to sheet, or the backlog. (2026-09-15) **Retired 2026-10-06** (decision 26): ownership is out of the product; the app tracks what was played, where it was played (decision 19), and what the user is thinking of playing.
20. **Hide what is empty.** No "Purchased: add" rows. Personal fields (purchased date, hours, notes) live behind a plus next to Details and appear only once filled. (2026-09-15)
21. **One scoreboard per source.** Critic and player scores come from one named source and are labeled; Steam reviews are a phrase ("Mostly positive"), never a number next to a metascore. Your own score appears only once you have ranked the game. (2026-09-15)
22. **Escape hatches visible, still secondary.** Center the comparison cards and pull the hatches up under them; no dead space between choice and exit. (2026-09-15)
23. **The prompt knows the genre.** When the pivot shares a genre with the new game, say so: "Which RPG did you enjoy more?" plus a visual cue. Otherwise the generic prompt. (2026-09-15) After each structure round, three friends, one task each, watch where they hesitate.
24. **Same loop, own product.** The ranking loop (tiers, comparisons, confirm) is shared by every medium. Everything around it (Home, the list tab, the detail page's actions, the moment of logging, the tab set) is designed from the medium's own use outward, in that medium's brief, and any reuse from games is argued there rather than assumed. (2026-09-22, replacing the 2026-09-16 wording "same skeleton, different facts": the media structure round drawn under it reused the games screens with the nouns swapped, and Rafa rejected that on 2026-09-22.)
25. **Awards are a fact, not a score.** Oscars, Emmys, the Booker and the like get one line on the detail page ("Won 6 Oscars · 10 nominations ›"), hidden when there are none, and never enter the ranking or the score line. (2026-09-16)
26. **Availability stands in for ownership where the thing is streamed.** For film and TV, "where to watch" from the catalog, with the services you pay for marked, replaces the owned-platforms fact; books keep ownership, by format. (2026-09-16)
27. **Progress is a pointer, and "up to date" is derived.** A series carries a season-and-episode pointer, a book a page; the app never asks the user to declare "caught up", it compares the pointer with what has aired. (2026-09-16)
28. **One time figure per medium.** Time to beat, runtime, total watch time, reading estimate: one number in the row and on the page, the detail behind a tap. (2026-09-16)
29. **Never crop box art.** A cover is drawn whole at its own ratio; the layout adapts to the cover, never the cover to the layout. Where a slot needs another shape, fetch an asset made for that shape (a square icon-style image, the way a Switch home tile is) rather than cutting the art; where none exists, the slot takes the cover's shape. The screen edge may clip a cover, the image never is. (2026-09-16)
30. **Every cover has an edge.** A hairline border and a soft shadow on every cover, in light and in dark, so dark art (Outer Wilds) does not sink into the dark canvas and white art does not sink into the light one. Drawn once, in the `Cover` primitive. (2026-09-16)
31. **Platforms are glyphs in line, names in menus.** On the game page a platform is a brand glyph (PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo, PC, Mac, Linux, iOS, Android), with a short suffix only when two platforms of one family are both present (PS4 and PS5), and the full name as the accessibility label. Full names appear where the reader is choosing from a list: the Ranking platform filter and the platforms sheet. Ownership shows one to three glyphs in line, none past three, and the tap opens the full list; "I have this game" carries no "on these platforms" wording. (2026-09-16)
32. **The active tab is unmistakable.** A weight change alone is too slight; the active tab reads at a glance, icon and label in the brand text color (`brand/text`: `#006822` in light, `#009B37` in dark). (2026-09-16; "in the medium color" until decision 29 was adopted in code on 2026-10-08, #76.)
33. **Progress is dots.** A step counter ("1 of 3") is replaced by dots, one per expected step, filled as answered, with the tier label under them. (2026-09-16)
34. **A watchlist is a pool, not a queue.** Films you want to watch carry no order, no rank numbers, and no drag handle; filters (your services, runtime, genre) and a sort do the work. The games backlog keeps its order because a game is a commitment. (2026-09-22)
35. **Watched is not ranked.** Logging a film (date, where, a note) is one step; ranking is offered after it, can be skipped, and is never implied by "I watched this". Imports and bulk entry never rank on their own: bulk entry never ranks, and the Letterboxd import marks films watched by default and places them from their stars only when the user turns that on and confirms the draft (decision 35, 2026-10-12). A watched, unranked film is a first-class state with a way back to ranking on its page and on the Ranking tab. (2026-09-22)
36. **Movies Home is rows of reasons behind one question.** Games Home is informational, because when you want to play you play. Movies Home shows films first: one big colored button, "What do you want to watch?", opens the question (what kind, how long, in your words) as a sheet; under it the page scrolls through rows of picks, and every row title is the reason you are seeing those films, from your own list: the watchlist first, then the genres you rank highest (the genre word alone), then "Because you loved *Film*". The engine chooses which rows appear, seven at most; a row with nothing to say is skipped. The poster is the card, with the service, genre, year and duration overlaid and no title. Services are the silent filter and the pill, never the lead and never a row; nothing on Home is the diary, the calendar, popularity or trending; "Tonight" is not a reason. (2026-09-22 as "Home is a finder for movies"; rewritten 2026-10-06 and decided 2026-10-07 from Rafa's letters on seven drawn rounds, decision 28. Rule 3's three blocks do not apply: the rows are as many as the list earns.)
37. **Streaming and theaters are separate.** The watchlist holds what you will stream; the calendar holds what you will see in theaters (the openings you care about, the date you are going) and the dates watchlist films reach a service. Which list a film sits in follows from its release state, never from a choice. (2026-09-22)
38. **A medium waits until its ranking unit is decided.** TV (season, episode, or show) and books are held; no frame or screen is drawn for a medium whose unit of judgment is undecided. (2026-09-22)

## Color direction (2026-09-15, for the direction round)

- The four medium colors are too bright as drawn, and four different hues risk diluting the brand the way Beli's single color never does. Direction to explore: one unifying treatment across all four (metallic or shiny, a shared finish), a logo that incorporates all four colors on the app icon and signed-out screen, and a logo that shifts to the active medium's color on each home.
- Tier colors are their own palette, not the medium colors: Beli's green / yellow / red framing extended to four steps (Loved, Liked, Fine, Didn't like). Ranking score badges use a gradient across that palette, as Beli does.
- Decided 2026-09-16: the tier palette is the one on the Direction round page (light #6AC56A, #A0BA39, #E49C12, #FF8274; dark #8AD989, #B7CF64, #F6B553, #FFA89D; near-black label text on every chip), and the labeled tier dividers in Ranking take the same colors. Score badges shade continuously across the palette by score.
- 2026-10-07, Rafa by voice: the per-medium colors "seemed cool" but detract from the brand identity; move to one color everywhere, leaning green but open, and be intentional about the pick. Claude's framework, evidence and math are in [../research/brand-color.md](../research/brand-color.md): one color yes; the green that passes white-text contrast is Xbox green and the green that avoids it is the Loved chip, so the surviving green is a deep blue-green at OKLCH hue 165 (pine `#007C59` light, `#00BA87` dark); Beli's own brand is a dark teal `#134F5C` with green-to-red kept for scores. Three candidates (pine, berry, ink) are drawn from [figma-kit/brand-color-round.md](../private/design/figma-kit/brand-color-round.md) before anything is decided. The tier palette is not reopened; the simulation that found Loved and Didn't-like identical under deuteranopia is held as a question.
- Decided 2026-10-07 on three Foundations boards (176:2, 180:2, 184:2): the brand is deep green `#006822`, the fill in both modes with white labels; brand text and icons lift to `#009B37` in dark mode (or `#64A86C`, his word owed); the tier palette is option A, four deep solids with white labels in both modes, Loved `#006822`, Liked `#5B6C00`, Fine `#865900`, Didn't like `#BB0A10`, superseding the 2026-09-16 palette above, with a tier icon on every chip (heart, thumbs up, meh, thumbs down) because the four deeps are near-identical under red-green color blindness. The score badges take the same four solids with white numerals; the tier divider is a line plus a chip, never tier-colored text on the canvas. B (Excel tints with same-hue deep text) and C (mid solids with black text) are kept for testing. The record is [../research/brand-color.md](../research/brand-color.md).

## Naming decided so far

- Tabs for games: Home, Search, Ranking, Next, Profile. Ranking is both tab and header. Next's header is "Up next." Confirmed 2026-09-15.
- Next tab segments, revised 2026-09-15: "Backlog" and "Want to play" overlap, so the model becomes Backlog (every released game you intend to play, owned or not) and Upcoming (unreleased games you want). Ownership is separate (rule 19). The Upcoming segment's label is not final.
- Tiers: Loved it, Liked it, It was fine, Didn't like it.
- Comparison prompt: "Which did you enjoy more?", decided 2026-09-16; with a shared genre, "Which {genre} did you enjoy more?"
- Next tab second segment: "Upcoming", decided 2026-09-16.
- Time to beat on the game page: one figure, "~25 h ›", with rushed and 100% behind the tap. Decided 2026-09-16.
- Per-medium labels, proposed 2026-09-16 in the media structure round (rule 18): the queue is Backlog / Want to watch / Want to watch / Want to read; in progress is Playing / (not shown for movies) / Watching / Reading; done is Played / Watched / Finished / Finished; dropped is Dropped / Didn't finish / Dropped / Didn't finish; the primary button is "I finished this" everywhere except movies, where it is "I watched this". The Next header stays "Up next" in every medium unless Rafa prefers a verb per medium.
- Movies, decided 2026-09-22 ([movies-brief.md](movies-brief.md)): the tab set is Home, Search, Ranking, Watchlist, Profile, with "Watchlist" replacing "Next" and its segments Watchlist | Calendar; "Didn't finish" is hidden for movies unless Rafa objects; the log sheet's buttons are "Save and rank" and "Save". TV keeps its total-hours figure ("~20 h") when it returns (Rafa, 2026-09-22).

## Data honesty, decided 2026-09-15

- Playtime, started date, and purchased date are always enterable by hand. Imported values (Steam) fill them when available and say so.
- The full ranking of which fact to show first on a backlog row waits until the app is in daily use.
- "Critic score" means Metacritic's metascore where Steam licenses it, otherwise OpenCritic or IGDB's aggregate. Metacritic's user score cannot be obtained legitimately; "player score" means Steam's review percentage or IGDB's user rating, labeled by source.
- Stored source names (2026-09-16): `external_scores.source` is one of `igdb_critics`, `igdb_users`, `steam` (score is the percent positive, count is total reviews; the phrase is derived on read, never stored), and `metacritic` (Valve's licensed metascore, score only). On-screen labels come from these names.
- Decided 2026-09-16: the Players figure is Steam's review phrase for Steam games, labeled "Steam"; a game without a Steam id shows IGDB's rating labeled "IGDB". Critics is Metacritic through Steam's licensed field, otherwise IGDB's aggregate until OpenCritic's official API is wired. Steam concurrent players join the game page for Steam games.
- Film and TV, proposed 2026-09-16 (media structure round, confirmed by [../research/film-tv-data-sources.md](../research/film-tv-data-sources.md)): the user score is TMDB's, labeled "TMDB"; IMDb's rating is not shown because its data is licensed for personal use only; Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic appear only as Wikidata's CC0 values, labeled with "via Wikidata, as of {date}" and without branding; nothing from OMDb. Books (confirmed by [../research/book-data-sources.md](../research/book-data-sources.md)): Hardcover's average is the one community rating a published app may show, labeled "Hardcover" with its count and hidden under about 100 ratings; never Goodreads, Amazon, StoryGraph, or Google Books. Awards come from Wikidata and are a fact, not a score (rule 25); series show wins only.

## Things Rafa specifically wants

- A zoomed-out collection view of every game played, in the spirit of Pokémon TCG Pocket's collection, good enough to share. Enters as a grid toggle on Ranking in V0; zoom and share polish later.
- Signed-out screen: the cover collage runs behind a translucent sheet so the games show through.
- A suggestion feature ("give me something to play from my likes and this genre") that lives outside Search, since Search is for finding a specific game. Later, and central for movies.
- A friends area on Profile with a taste-similarity score, in the social phase.
- The confirm-placement step, which Beli does not have, kept as a differentiator.
- The tier step as a bottom sheet over the game page (four tiers, each with an icon and a color); tapping a tier expands the sheet to full screen and the comparisons begin inside it. One continuous surface from "I finished this" to placement.
- Several ways to say "I finished this": the big button on the game page, and a long press on the Playing tile on Home for the quick version (a plain tap opens the game page).
