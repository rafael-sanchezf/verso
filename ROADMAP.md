# Roadmap

Verso is a recommender across media, fed by a Beli-style ranking loop: you place what you played or watched in a ranked list through a few head-to-head comparisons, and the engine reads that list, your backlog and the time you have to say what to spend tonight on and why. This file is the source of truth for scope and decisions: the end state and principles, the version plan from V0 to V4, the record of what has landed, the dated decisions and the data model, with the milestones, the open items, the venture side and the running log kept in [docs/private/ROADMAP-private.md](docs/private/ROADMAP-private.md), which never leaves the working repo. Read [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) for how to work in the repo.

## End state

Verso is a recommender across media that people pay for, and a Beli-style ranking loop is how it learns (decision 21, 2026-09-30). You log what you played or watched and place it in your ranked list through a few head-to-head comparisons; that answer, given after you finish, is a satisfaction label nobody else collects. The engine reads it with your backlog, drops, notes, platforms and services, the time you have, and what you import, and tells you what to spend tonight on and why, in your own terms; with friends, what the room should watch. Games and movies first; TV and books when their units are decided. Around the loop sits the pulled-in data (cover art, critic and user scores, playtime, player counts, time to beat, where to play and where to watch), which is what makes a pick actionable. The tracker and the social layer are free; the engine is the paid capability. Nobody's personal data is ever sold.

Build order is games to completion first, then other media. The first version is built with the end state in mind, so the data model, the ranking engine, and the UI shell are media-agnostic even while only games are exposed. Since 2026-09-22 movies are built beside games in V0, and since 2026-09-30 both ship in the beta and at launch (decisions 18 and 23).

The value has two layers (Rafa, 2026-09-16). On the surface, a catalog of everything you like across media. Underneath, suggestions built on your rankings and comparison log: personal ("what should I play next, in this genre, in the time I have") and for a group ("a film for the two of us tonight," "a multiplayer game for the four of us"). The ranking loop is what makes the second layer possible, so it stays the product and the suggestions are its payoff. On 2026-09-30 the layers swapped (decision 21): the suggestions became the product and the ranking its sensor. Ideas not yet scheduled are in [docs/feature-ideas.md](docs/feature-ideas.md).

## Principles

- **The engine is the product, and ranking is its best sensor.** The comparison loop, the backlog, and the social layer exist to feed it, and they are free. (2026-09-30, decision 21. It replaces the principle "Ranking is the product. The comparison-based ranker and the backlog are what the app is for. Everything else is enrichment.")
- **Never sold, free or paid.** No sale or cross-context sharing of personal data on any tier; data products are aggregate only, built to California's de-identification standard; no SDK that shares data across apps. (2026-09-30, decision 21.)
- **The first session is the trial.** A stranger must reach ten ranked items and one convincing pick with its reason before leaving, and every onboarding decision is judged by that, because most trial cancellations happen on day one. (2026-09-30, the market research's second lesson.)
- **Media-agnostic core.** Items, entries, rankings, and lists must not assume "game." Medium-specific fields live in a per-medium extension, not in the core tables.
- **Each medium is its own product.** Rule 24, "same loop, own product" (2026-09-22): the ranking loop is shared by every medium, and everything around it (Home, the list tab, the detail page's actions, the moment of logging, the tab set) is designed from the medium's own use outward, in that medium's brief ([docs/design/ui-principles.md](docs/design/ui-principles.md), [docs/design/movies-brief.md](docs/design/movies-brief.md)). A principle since 2026-09-30.
- **Own the user's data, borrow the catalog.** Catalog metadata comes from external sources and can be refreshed. Rankings, statuses, notes, and play history are ours and must never depend on an external ID staying stable.
- **Best-effort integrations, honest UI.** Where a platform has no official API (PlayStation, Nintendo), it is acceptable to ship a fragile or manual path, as long as the UI says so and the app still works without it.
- **Ship narrow, ship real.** V0 is a tool one person uses daily. It does not need a feed or social features, but it does run on a real backend from day one so the data model and sync story never need a migration. Updated 2026-09-30: V0 is now the private beta, 30 to 100 people rather than one, and it still has no feed.
- **Design before build.** UI decisions are made in Figma and reviewed there before any screen is coded. The app is meant to be published, so the interface is intentional rather than whatever a first implementation produced.

## V3: TV (movies moved to V0 on 2026-09-22)

## Decisions

All dated. Nothing here is reopened without being asked.

1. **Ranking algorithm.** Decided 2026-09-07: Option A. Beli's flow kept intact: pick a tier, then binary-search insertion within the tier, score read off position. Four tiers (loved, liked, fine, did not like), each owning a fixed band of the 0-10 scale. Two escape hatches ("too close to call" places directly below the compared game; "can't compare" swaps the pivot without narrowing the range, and two in a row end the search at the range midpoint). A confirm step after insertion shows the new game between its neighbors with up and down nudges. Every comparison is stored as an event so a later move to a latent model (Option C) is a recompute, not a migration. Full explanation in [docs/design/ranking-algorithm.md](docs/design/ranking-algorithm.md).
2. **Comparing across genres.** Decided 2026-09-07: genre is a pivot preference inside Option A, not a hard filter. At each step of the search, if the current range contains a game sharing a genre with the new one within a threshold distance of the exact midpoint, ask about that game; otherwise ask about the midpoint. One global list per medium is preserved and genre views are filters over it. The threshold is deliberately unset and will be tuned by using the app. Tuning 2026-09-16: "Indie" is ignored for genre matching, since it says who made a game rather than what kind of game it is (`ignoredGenres` in the engine options). Option B (per-genre buckets) stays on the shelf.
3. **The comparison question.** Flickchart's users found that mixing "which is better" with "which do I love" destabilizes rankings. Decided 2026-09-16: "Which did you enjoy more?", printed on the screen; when the pivot shares a genre, "Which {genre} did you enjoy more?" (work queue item 10). The wording stays a parameter in the engine, so a later change is one constant.
4. **Platform.** Decided 2026-09-07: mobile-first. End state is iOS, Android, and a website. Decided 2026-09-08: one Expo codebase (Expo Router universal app) in a pnpm monorepo, with a separate Next.js site added later only when public indexable game pages matter. SwiftUI rejected because it doubles every feature once the social layer lands. See [docs/design/tech-stack.md](docs/design/tech-stack.md). UI is designed in Figma before it is built, see [docs/design/figma-workflow.md](docs/design/figma-workflow.md).

The app talks to Supabase directly; Next.js is not the data layer. Zero cost while only you use it, 25 USD a month from TestFlight. Convex is the runner-up. See [docs/design/tech-stack.md](docs/design/tech-stack.md).

## Data model

Implemented 2026-09-09 in `supabase/migrations/20260909000000_core_schema.sql` and extended by the migrations in the work queue. The sketch below is the shape; the tables are `media_items`, `game_details`, `external_scores`, `user_entries`, `ranking_entries`, `comparisons`, `platform_links`, `lists`, `list_items`, `profiles`, `player_counts`, and `game_availability`, `steam_library`, and `steam_link_states`, every one of them behind Row Level Security with a policy test.

- **MediaItem**: id, medium (game, movie, tv), title, year, release date, cover image id, external ids (igdb, steam, tmdb later), a metadata blob the mirror merges into, last refreshed. One row per thing that can be ranked. Written only by the service role; readable by signed-in users, and by nobody else except through `popular_games`.
- **GameDetails** (MovieDetails, TvDetails later): medium-specific columns keyed to MediaItem. Platforms, genres, the time-to-beat triple, developer, summary.
- **UserEntry**: user, media item, status, date added, started, finished, purchased, notes, playtime minutes and their source, owned platforms, backlog position.
- **Ranking**: user, medium, tier, media item, position, placed at, updated at. The 0-10 score is derived at read time from position and tier size, never stored.
- **Comparison**: user, medium, item A, item B, outcome, context, timestamp. Every head-to-head answer is logged, append-only, so the ranking can be rebuilt or migrated to a different algorithm later. This is the decision that most protects the future.
- **ExternalScore**: media item, source (igdb_critics, igdb_users, steam, metacritic, opencritic later), score, count, fetched at.
- **PlatformLink**: user, platform (steam, psn, nintendo), external user id, token reference, last synced, sync status. Empty until V1.
- **List**: user, name, ordered media items, shared with. Owner-only until V2.
- Proposed for V3 and V4 (2026-09-16, [docs/design/media-brief.md](docs/design/media-brief.md)): `movie_details`, `tv_details`, `book_details` as further extensions; a progress pointer and `owned_formats` on UserEntry; an **Award** table (media item, award, category, year, result won or nominated, Wikidata id, fetched at); watch providers per item and region; a user settings row (region, streaming provider ids); `medium` gains `book`; the status values renamed to `queued`, `in_progress`, `done`, `dropped`.
- Proposed for V0's movies (2026-09-22, [docs/design/movies-brief.md](docs/design/movies-brief.md)): a **WatchLog** diary table (user, media item, watched on, place, note; owner-only) whose first row marks the entry done; `planned_on` on UserEntry for the ticketed date; the settings row (region, streaming provider ids) pulled forward from V3; the watched-unranked count as a read shared with bulk entry.
- Proposed for the recommendation engine (2026-09-24, [docs/design/recommendation-engine.md](docs/design/recommendation-engine.md#schema-sketch-proposed)): `item_prior` (catalog-like), `user_similarity` (service role only; friends read their Match through a function), `predictions` and `prediction_log` (owner-only), `taste_summaries`, `suggestion_requests` (the meter from the feature sketch), `group_sessions` with members, guests, picks, and outcomes; later `item_tags`, `item_links`, IGDB's multiplayer modes, and a pgvector column on `media_items`.
- Proposed for the beta (2026-09-30): an `events` table for the funnel (owner-only rows), `profiles.media` for the questionnaire's chosen media and `profiles.ai_consent_at` for the third-party model permission, enforced in the suggest function (2026-10-01, [docs/design/onboarding-brief.md](docs/design/onboarding-brief.md)), and the import tables the Letterboxd import needs.
- Built for the Letterboxd import (2026-10-12, `supabase/migrations/20261012130000_letterboxd_import.sql`, decision 35): `letterboxd_imports` (user, when, file names, counts, diary rows written) and `letterboxd_import_films` (import, user, Letterboxd URI, title, year, rating, watched dates, rewatches, matched media item, outcome of ranked, watched, unmatched or skipped, tier and position), both owner-only with policy tests; `match_letterboxd_films` and `apply_letterboxd_import`, both `security invoker`. Watched dates become `watch_log` rows; an imported place is marked with the existing `placed_by`, so no new column on `ranking_entries`.
